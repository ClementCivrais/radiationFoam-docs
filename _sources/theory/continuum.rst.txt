Continuum radiation
===================

Besides the lines, a partially ionised gas emits and absorbs over wide
wavelength ranges through two **continuum** processes:

* **bound-free**: photoionisation of an atom in level :math:`i`,
  :math:`\mathrm{A}(i) + h\nu \to \mathrm{A}^+ + e^-`, and its reverse,
  radiative recombination of an electron onto level :math:`i`;
* **free-free**: bremsstrahlung, the emission of a free electron
  deflected in the Coulomb field of an ion, and its reverse, inverse
  bremsstrahlung.

Both are off by default. Sources: ``src/radiation/continuum``
(``continuumModel``, ``continuumProcesses``, ``boundFreePhysical``,
``freeFreePhysical``); the classes and their keywords are in
:doc:`/classes/continuum`.

Selection
---------

The entry ``continuum`` of ``system/radiationProperties`` (top level,
overridable per species) takes two values:

* ``none`` (default): no continuum; the spectra are those of the
  bound-bound classes alone.
* ``physical``: for every atomic species (``model atomicLines``)

  - a ``boundFreePhysical`` process if its ``continuumCoeffs`` give an
    ``ionSpecies`` (the species one charge higher, which must itself be a
    radiating species of the case);
  - a ``freeFreePhysical`` process if its ``chargeNumber`` is 1 or more
    (the species is an ion).

Molecules have no continuum: a species entry ``continuum physical`` on a
non-atomic species stops with an error; the global entry skips them.
For N, O, N\ :sup:`+` and O\ :sup:`+`, the neutrals carry the bound-free
processes (with ``ionSpecies N+`` and ``O+``) and the ions carry the
free-free processes.

The coefficients of all processes are added to the bound-bound
:math:`j_\lambda` and :math:`\kappa_\lambda` at every grid point
(``continuumProcesses::addEmissionAbsorption``), before the transfer
(:doc:`/theory/transport`). There is no profile and no placement: the
continuum is evaluated directly at each grid wavelength.

Both processes use the free electrons of the cell state
(:math:`n_e`, :math:`T_e`, Maxwellian at :math:`T_e`). A cell with
:math:`T_e \le 0` has no continuum, whatever :math:`n_e`.

Bound-free continuum
--------------------

Photoionisation cross sections
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The process is **level-resolved**: every bound level :math:`i` of the
atom (the levels of its transition file) has its own cross section
:math:`\sigma_i(\nu)` [m\ :sup:`2`] and its own population :math:`n_i`
from the population model (:doc:`/theory/populations`), the same
populations as for the lines. A photon ionises level :math:`i` only
above the threshold

.. math::

   h\nu \ge E_b - E_i ,

where :math:`E_b` is the bound limit of the population model (the
lowered ionisation limit, the same as for the lines; if the model gives
:math:`E_b \le 0` or :math:`E_b` above the ionisation energy, the
ionisation energy is used). Levels with :math:`E_i \ge E_b` are not
bound and are skipped.

Two cross-section models are available (entry ``crossSection``):

**hydrogenic** (Kramers). The cross section of a hydrogen-like level of
effective principal quantum number :math:`n_\mathrm{eff}` (Zel'dovich
and Raizer 1966, as written by Johnston 2006, Eqs. 3.34-3.35, p. 70):

.. math::

   \sigma_i(\nu) = C_K\,\frac{Z^{4}\,G}{\nu^{3}\,n_\mathrm{eff}^{5}},
   \qquad
   C_K = \frac{64\,\pi^{4}\,m_e\,e^{10}}
              {3\sqrt{3}\,c\,h^{6}\,(4\pi\varepsilon_0)^{5}},

.. math::

   n_\mathrm{eff} = Z\sqrt{\frac{R_y}{I - E_i}},
   \qquad
   R_y = \frac{m_e\,e^{4}}{8\,\varepsilon_0^{2}\,h^{2}},
   \qquad
   Z = \texttt{chargeNumber} + 1 ,

with :math:`I` the (unlowered) ionisation energy of the species and
:math:`G` the bound-free Gaunt factor (entry ``gauntFactor``, default 1:
the Kramers value; the database holds no referenced Gaunt factors).
:math:`C_K = 2.815\times10^{25}` m\ :sup:`2` Hz\ :sup:`3`; for a
hydrogen ground state (:math:`Z = 1`, :math:`n_\mathrm{eff} = 1`) it
gives 7.9 Mb at threshold (1 Mb = :math:`10^{-22}` m\ :sup:`2`).

**tabulated**. Per level, from a photoionisation file
(``database/atoms/N_I_photoionisation.dat``, ``O_I_photoionisation.dat``),
generated by ``database/sources/build_photoionisation.py`` from
Johnston (2006), Appendix B (TOPbase fits). Each level takes one of three
forms:

* ``steps``: a step model, :math:`\sigma` constant on
  :math:`[h\nu_A, h\nu_B)` (Tables B-1, B-3). Below the first step
  (between the lowered threshold and the first tabulated energy) the
  first step value is used; above the last step, :math:`\sigma = 0` (the
  end of Johnston's tables, 18.9-19.0 eV);
* ``power``: the fit (Johnston 2006, Eq. 3.36)

  .. math::

     \sigma_i = \sigma_\mathrm{th}
       \left(\frac{h\nu_\mathrm{th}}{h\nu}\right)^{\theta}

  (Tables B-2, B-4), with no upper cut-off;
* ``hydrogenic``: the Kramers form above, for the levels outside
  Johnston's level model.

Each level of the database takes the cross section of the Johnston level
group it belongs to (the assignment is listed in the header of the data
file).

Emission from absorption: the Milne relation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The code does not use a separate recombination cross section. It
obtains the recombination emission from the photoionisation cross
section by **detailed balance** level by level (the Milne relation,
e.g. Rybicki and Lightman 1979). In equilibrium at :math:`T_e`, the
population of level :math:`i` in balance with the ion ground level and
the free electrons is the Saha-Boltzmann value

.. math::

   S_i = n_e\,\frac{n_{+0}}{g_{+0}}\,\frac{g_i}{2}\,\Lambda^{3}
         \exp\!\left(\frac{E_b - E_i}{k T_e}\right),
   \qquad
   \Lambda^{3} = \left(\frac{h^{2}}{2\pi m_e k T_e}\right)^{3/2},

where :math:`n_{+0}`, :math:`g_{+0}` are the population and degeneracy
of the ion ground level (level 0 of the ``ionSpecies``, from the
population model), :math:`g_i` the degeneracy of level :math:`i`, and
the factor 2 the spin degeneracy of the free electron. The spectral
coefficients per unit frequency are then (Johnston 2006,
Eqs. 3.24-3.30, pp. 68-69)

.. math::

   j_\nu = \frac{2 h \nu^{3}}{c^{2}}
           \sum_i \sigma_i(\nu)\,S_i\,e^{-h\nu/kT_e},

.. math::

   \kappa_\nu = \sum_i \sigma_i(\nu)
     \left(n_i - S_i\,e^{-h\nu/kT_e}\right),

where the stimulated recombination is written as a negative absorption.
Two checks follow directly:

* if the populations are the Saha-Boltzmann ones (:math:`n_i = S_i`),
  :math:`\kappa_\nu = \sum_i \sigma_i S_i (1 - e^{-h\nu/kT_e})` and

  .. math::

     \frac{j_\nu}{\kappa_\nu} = \frac{2h\nu^{3}}{c^{2}}
       \frac{1}{e^{h\nu/kT_e} - 1} = B_\nu(T_e),

  so an optically thick, equilibrium layer radiates as a black body;
* if the populations come from a nonequilibrium model (e.g. a DSMC cell
  distribution), the absorption uses the actual :math:`n_i` while the
  emission depends only on the ion and the electrons, as it should for
  recombination. :math:`\kappa_\nu` may then be negative (gain); it is
  kept with its sign.

The emission per unit wavelength is :math:`j_\lambda = j_\nu\,c/\lambda^2`;
the absorption coefficient :math:`\kappa` [m\ :sup:`-1`] is the same in
both representations.

Free-free continuum
-------------------

For an ion of charge :math:`Z` (entry ``chargeNumber`` :math:`\ge 1`)
and number density :math:`n_+` (the density of that species in the cell
state), Kramers' emission coefficient of Maxwellian electrons is
(Johnston 2006, Eq. 3.32, p. 69; Zel'dovich and Raizer 1966; Kramers
1923), in SI:

.. math::

   j_\nu = C_{ff}\,Z^{2}\,G_{ff}\,n_e\,n_+\,T_e^{-1/2}\,
           e^{-h\nu/kT_e},

.. math::

   C_{ff} = \frac{8}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\,
            \frac{e^{6}}{(4\pi\varepsilon_0)^{3}\,m_e^{3/2}\,c^{3}\,k^{1/2}}
          = 5.444\times10^{-52}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{3}\,sr^{-1}\,Hz^{-1}\,K^{1/2}},

with :math:`G_{ff}` the free-free Gaunt factor (entry ``gauntFreeFree``,
default 1). The absorption follows from Kirchhoff's law. This is exact
for Maxwellian electrons and needs no assumption on the bound levels,
because both directions involve only free electrons:

.. math::

   \kappa_\nu = \frac{j_\nu}{B_\nu(T_e)}
     = C_{ff}\,Z^{2}\,G_{ff}\,n_e\,n_+\,T_e^{-1/2}\,
       \frac{c^{2}}{2h\nu^{3}}\left(1 - e^{-h\nu/kT_e}\right),

stimulated emission included. The code evaluates
:math:`1 - e^{-x}` as ``-expm1(-x)``, accurate for small :math:`x`.
Electron-neutral bremsstrahlung is not included.

Constants
---------

Both classes use the exact SI 2019 values of :math:`h`, :math:`k`,
:math:`c`, :math:`e` and the CODATA 2018 values of :math:`m_e` and
:math:`\varepsilon_0`, written in the class headers.

Outputs
-------

In ``singleCell`` mode, an active continuum writes
``postProcessing/radiationFoam/continuum.csv`` with the emission and
absorption of every process (named ``<model>_<species>``, e.g.
``boundFreePhysical_N``) and prints their integrals in the log. In
``shockTube1D`` mode the continuum is part of the total coefficients
(:doc:`/classes/transport`).
