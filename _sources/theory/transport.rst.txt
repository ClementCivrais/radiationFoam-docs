Radiative transfer
==================

The radiators (:doc:`/theory/atomic_lines`,
:doc:`/theory/diatomic_bands`, :doc:`/theory/continuum`) give, in each
cell, the spectral emission coefficient :math:`j_\lambda`
[W m\ :sup:`-3` sr\ :sup:`-1` m\ :sup:`-1`] and the spectral absorption
coefficient :math:`\kappa_\lambda` [m\ :sup:`-1`], summed over all
species and processes. The **transport model** turns these local
coefficients into the quantity that is measured or that leaves the gas:
a spectral radiance :math:`I_\lambda`
[W m\ :sup:`-2` sr\ :sup:`-1` m\ :sup:`-1`].

radiationFoam has two transport models, selected by the entry ``mode``
of ``system/radiationProperties``
(``src/radiation/transport``, :doc:`/classes/transport`):

* ``singleCell``: one cell, no transfer;
* ``shockTube1D``: the cells of a 1-D shock-tube profile, each a uniform
  slab across the tube.

The application ``radiationFoam``
(``applications/solvers/radiationFoam/radiationFoam.C``) builds the
radiating species, the population model and the continuum processes,
selects the transport model by ``mode`` and calls its ``solve()``. It
runs in serial (``argList::noParallel``).

The radiative transfer equation
-------------------------------

Along a ray of direction :math:`\mathbf{s}`, a beam gains energy by
emission and loses energy by absorption:

.. math::

   \frac{\mathrm{d}I_\lambda}{\mathrm{d}s} = j_\lambda - \kappa_\lambda\,I_\lambda .

Scattering is neglected (a good approximation for a gas). Here
:math:`\kappa_\lambda` is **net of stimulated emission**: the induced
emission, proportional to :math:`I_\lambda` like the absorption, is
counted as a negative absorption. In a medium of uniform :math:`j` and
:math:`\kappa`, the ratio :math:`S_\lambda = j_\lambda/\kappa_\lambda`
is the source function; in local thermodynamic equilibrium it is the
Planck function :math:`B_\lambda(T)`.

Uniform slab
~~~~~~~~~~~~

For a uniform layer of thickness :math:`D` with no radiation entering
it (:math:`I_\lambda = 0` at :math:`s = 0`), the equation integrates to

.. math::

   I_\lambda(D) = \frac{j_\lambda}{\kappa_\lambda}
     \left(1 - e^{-\kappa_\lambda D}\right)
   = -\frac{j_\lambda}{\kappa_\lambda}\,
     \mathrm{expm1}(-\kappa_\lambda D) .

Its two limits, with the optical thickness :math:`\tau = \kappa_\lambda D`:

* **optically thin**, :math:`\tau \ll 1`: :math:`I_\lambda \to j_\lambda D`.
  Every photon emitted in the layer reaches the observer;
* **optically thick**, :math:`\tau \gg 1`: :math:`I_\lambda \to
  j_\lambda/\kappa_\lambda`. The observer sees only the last
  :math:`1/\kappa_\lambda` of the layer, and in equilibrium the radiance
  saturates at the black body :math:`B_\lambda(T)`.

The code evaluates the slab formula with ``std::expm1``. For small
:math:`\tau` the naive form :math:`1 - e^{-\tau}` loses its digits to
round-off (relative error 2e-5 at :math:`\tau = 10^{-12}`, 11 % at
:math:`10^{-16}`, by the script below), while ``-expm1(-tau)`` is
accurate to round-off for every :math:`\tau > 0`. :math:`\kappa = 0`
exactly gives :math:`I = jD`. A negative :math:`\kappa` (gain, an
inverted pair of levels) is kept: the same formula then gives
:math:`I = (j/|\kappa|)(e^{|\kappa|D} - 1) > jD`.

   :width: 55%

   Figure 1. Slab radiance over its optically thin value,
   :math:`I_\lambda/(j_\lambda D) = -\mathrm{expm1}(-\tau)/\tau`
   (present), and its thin and thick limits. Script:
   ``_static/figures/transport/fig_slab.py``.

singleCell: emission and absorption of one cell
-----------------------------------------------

``singleCell`` computes :math:`j_\lambda` and :math:`\kappa_\lambda` of
one cell state on one spectral grid and writes them
(``postProcessing/radiationFoam/spectrum.csv``) with the level
populations. It performs **no transfer**: the cell is a point. It
is the mode for checking the spectral coefficients against a baseline
code or a hand calculation, and for building the coefficients that a
user then integrates over a path of their choice. The log reports, per
species and process, the emitted power
:math:`4\pi\int j_\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\lambda` [W m\ :sup:`-3`] and
:math:`\int\kappa_\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\lambda` over the window. The
optically thin radiance of a uniform layer of thickness :math:`D` is
:math:`j_\lambda D`; the slab formula above applies with the written
:math:`\kappa_\lambda`.

shockTube1D: radiance across a shock tube
-----------------------------------------

Geometry
~~~~~~~~

In a shock-tube experiment such as EAST (Brandis and Cruden 2017) a
camera looks **across** the tube, perpendicular to its axis, through
windows in the tube wall, and images the radiance as a function of the
position :math:`x` along the axis. The flow behind the shock is, to a
first approximation, one-dimensional: its state depends on :math:`x`
only.

``shockTube1D`` takes a 1-D profile of cell states along :math:`x` (a
DSMC solution or a macro file, :doc:`/classes/profiles`) and treats
**every cell as an independent uniform slab** of thickness :math:`D`,
the tube diameter (entry ``diameter``). At each wavelength of each
spectral window:

.. math::

   I_\lambda(x_k) =
   \begin{cases}
     -\dfrac{j_\lambda(x_k)}{\kappa_\lambda(x_k)}\,
       \mathrm{expm1}\!\left(-\kappa_\lambda(x_k)\,D\right),
       & \kappa_\lambda \ne 0,\\[2ex]
     j_\lambda(x_k)\,D, & \kappa_\lambda = 0 .
   \end{cases}

This is the line-of-sight radiance through the tube at the axial
position of cell :math:`k`, under three assumptions:

* the gas is uniform across the tube along the line of sight (no
  boundary layer at the walls, no radial variation);
* no radiation enters the line of sight at the far wall: the walls
  neither emit nor reflect;
* there is **no transfer between cells along the tube**: a photon
  emitted in one cell is not absorbed by its neighbours. The radiance of
  cell :math:`k` depends only on the state of cell :math:`k`.

The line of sight is a diameter of the tube, so :math:`D` is its full
length; the chord of a ray off the axis would be shorter, and is not
modelled.

Mapping the profile onto the tube
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The profile cells are sorted by increasing :math:`x`
(``shockTubeProfile``) and numbered :math:`k = 0 \dots N-1` in that
order. Each cell gives the number density and the translational,
rotational, vibrational and electronic temperatures of every radiating
species, :math:`n_e` and :math:`T_e`; with ``populationModel
boltzmannTemperatures`` the level populations are the Boltzmann
re-expansion of these temperatures (:doc:`/theory/populations`).

With ``populationModel cellDistribution`` the level populations come
instead from the per-cell distribution files written by the DSMC
function object ``dsmcRadiationDistribution`` (directory with an index
``cells`` and files ``cell<k>``; :doc:`/guide/dsmcfoam_coupling`). Each
distribution cell is matched to the **nearest profile cell** along the
tube (component ``direction`` of its cell centre), within a tolerance of
``matchTolerance`` (default 1e-3) times the smallest profile cell
spacing; that profile cell gives the translational temperatures, the
densities and the electrons used by the line shapes. The cell index
:math:`k` is then that of the distribution file.

The position of the shock is not changed: :math:`x` is the coordinate of
the DSMC case or macro file. The shock front, and the distance behind
it, are determined only by the optional instrument model, which finds
:math:`x_0` from the measured band profile or takes a fixed position
(:doc:`/theory/instrument`).

Band and window integrals
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

For each cell, the radiance is integrated over each band :math:`[a, b]`
(entry ``bands``, in nm) and over each whole window by the rectangle
rule on the grid points:

.. math::

   I_\mathrm{band}(x_k) = \sum_{\lambda_i \in [a, b]}
     I_\lambda(x_k, \lambda_i)\,\Delta\lambda
   \quad [\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}],

a grid point on a band edge (within :math:`10^{-6}\Delta\lambda`) being
inside the band. With the bin placement of the lines
(:doc:`/theory/line_shapes`), a point stands for the bin
:math:`(\lambda_{i-1}, \lambda_i]`. The emitted power
:math:`4\pi\sum_i j_\lambda\,\Delta\lambda` [W m\ :sup:`-3`] of each
window is written as well.

The measured quantities of EAST (instrument line shape, spatial
resolution, shock-front criterion, nonequilibrium metric) are applied
to this raw radiance field after the cell loop by the instrument model
(:doc:`/theory/instrument`); the raw outputs are unchanged.

Escape factor of a uniform cylinder (DSMC side)
-----------------------------------------------

The radiationFoam repository contains only the two transport models
above: one-way (no feedback to the flow), with no transfer between
cells. It contains no escape factor, photon Monte Carlo or ray tracing.

A **local escape factor** is implemented in the DSMC repository
(dsmcFoam+), in the radiative decay class ``spontaneousEmission``
(``src/lagrangian/DSMC/radiationCoupling/gasGasReactions/spontaneousEmission``
there), where the radiative decay of the atomic levels removes energy
from the gas during the DSMC run. A photon emitted in a line can be
re-absorbed before it leaves the gas; the escape factor
:math:`\Lambda` is the fraction that escapes, and the effective decay
rate of the line is :math:`\Lambda A`. The DSMC class takes
:math:`\Lambda` for a **uniform infinite cylinder** of radius :math:`R`
(the tube radius, entry ``radius`` of its ``escape`` dictionary):

* the mean escape probability of photons emitted uniformly and
  isotropically in the cylinder, of absorption coefficient
  :math:`\kappa`,

  .. math::

     P_\mathrm{cyl}(\tau_R) = \frac{2}{R^{2}}\int_0^R r\,\mathrm{d}r\;
       \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \mathrm{Ki}_2\!\left(\kappa\,s(r,\varphi)\right)
       \mathrm{d}\varphi,
     \qquad \tau_R = \kappa R,

  with :math:`s` the in-plane path to the wall and
  :math:`\mathrm{Ki}_2` the Bickley function (the polar angle
  integrated); :math:`P_\mathrm{cyl}(0) = 1` and
  :math:`P_\mathrm{cyl} \to 1/(2\tau_R)` for large :math:`\tau_R`;
* the line escape factor :math:`\Lambda(\tau_0, a)`, the integral of
  :math:`P_\mathrm{cyl}` over the Voigt line profile, with
  :math:`\tau_0` the optical radius at line centre and :math:`a` the
  Voigt damping parameter, tabulated in ``escapeCylinder.table``.

The pure-geometry quadrature is
``src/lagrangian/DSMC/radiation/database/sources/escape_cylinder.py``
of the DSMC repository. The coupling design of the two codes (one-way
transport in 2-D and 3-D, the radiation-to-DSMC feedback) is described
in ``docs/RADIATION_COUPLING_SPEC.md`` of this repository; the parts of
it that are not listed above are plans, not code of radiationFoam. See
:doc:`/guide/dsmcfoam_coupling` for what the two codes exchange today.
