Atomic bound-bound lines
========================

An atom or atomic ion radiates in lines: a transition from an upper level
:math:`u` to a lower level :math:`l` emits a photon of wavelength
:math:`\lambda_0 = hc/(E_u - E_l)`, and the reverse transition absorbs one.
The radiator ``atomicLines``
(``src/radiation/radiators/derived/atomicLines``) computes the emission and
absorption of every such line of a species from a list of levels and
lines, with the level populations of the population model
(:doc:`/theory/populations`) and a Voigt profile per line
(:doc:`/theory/line_shapes`). Class and keywords: :doc:`/classes/radiators`.

Data per level and per line
---------------------------

The atomic file of the database (:doc:`/theory/database`) gives, per
level, its statistical weight :math:`g = 2J+1`, its energy :math:`E`
(cm\ :sup:`-1`) and an index; and, per line,

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 22 78

   * - Quantity
     - Meaning
   * - :math:`\lambda_0`
     - line centre, vacuum wavelength (Ångström in the file; the NIST ASD
       Ritz wavelength)
   * - :math:`l`, :math:`u`
     - indices of the lower and upper levels
   * - :math:`g_l^{\mathrm{line}}`, :math:`g_u^{\mathrm{line}}`
     - statistical weights of the two levels as given with the line
   * - :math:`A_{ul}`
     - Einstein coefficient for spontaneous emission [s\ :sup:`-1`]
   * - :math:`w_S`
     - Stark half width at half maximum [Å] at :math:`T_e = 10^4` K and
       :math:`n_e = 10^{16}` cm\ :sup:`-3`

The line centre is taken from the file, not recomputed from the level
energies. Lines are electric-dipole (E1) lines only.

Einstein coefficients and oscillator strengths
----------------------------------------------

The three Einstein coefficients of a line are linked by detailed balance.
:math:`A_{ul}` is the probability per unit time that an atom in :math:`u`
decays spontaneously to :math:`l`; absorption and stimulated emission
follow from it, which is why the file needs :math:`A_{ul}` only. The
absorption oscillator strength often quoted in tables is

.. math::

   f_{lu} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 m_e c\,\lambda_0^2}{2\pi e^2}\,
            \frac{g_u}{g_l}\,A_{ul},

the relation the code uses in its comments to convert widths written
with :math:`f_{lu}` (e.g. the resonance width below). The database stores
:math:`A_{ul}`, not :math:`f_{lu}`.

Emission and absorption of one line
-----------------------------------

With :math:`n_u`, :math:`n_l` the level populations [m\ :sup:`-3`] from the
population model and :math:`g(u)`, :math:`g(l)` the weights of the level
list, the line-integrated coefficients are (``atomicLines.C``)

.. math::

   \epsilon_{ul} = \frac{h c}{4\pi}\,
     \frac{g_u^{\mathrm{line}}}{g(u)}\,
     \frac{n_u A_{ul}}{\lambda_0}
   \quad [\mathrm{W\,m^{-3}\,sr^{-1}}],

.. math::

   k_{ul} = \frac{\lambda_0^4 A_{ul}}{8\pi c}
     \left(\frac{g_u^{\mathrm{line}}}{g(l)}\,n_l
         - \frac{g_u^{\mathrm{line}}}{g(u)}\,n_u\right)
   \quad [\mathrm{m^{-1}\,m}],

and the spectral coefficients on the grid are

.. math::

   j_\lambda(\lambda_i) \mathrel{+}= \max(0, \epsilon_{ul})\,\phi_i,
   \qquad
   \kappa_\lambda(\lambda_i) \mathrel{+}= k_{ul}\,\phi_i,

with :math:`\phi_i` [m\ :sup:`-1`] the bin-integrated, area-normalised
line profile at grid point :math:`i`.

Why these forms. The emission is the number of decays per unit volume and
time, :math:`n_u A_{ul}`, times the photon energy :math:`hc/\lambda_0`,
shared equally over :math:`4\pi` sr. The absorption is the absorption rate
minus the stimulated-emission rate, each written with :math:`A_{ul}`
through the Einstein relations; with :math:`f_{lu}` it reads

.. math::

   k_{ul} = \frac{e^2\lambda_0^2}{4\varepsilon_0 m_e c^2}\,f_{lu}
     \left(n_l - \frac{g_l}{g_u}\,n_u\right),

the classical form :math:`\pi r_e \lambda_0^2 f_{lu}\,n_l` corrected for
stimulated emission (:math:`r_e` the classical electron radius).

Stimulated emission and line gain
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The second term of :math:`k_{ul}` is stimulated emission; :math:`k_{ul}`
is the **net** absorption. When the populations per state are inverted,
:math:`n_u/g_u > n_l/g_l`, :math:`k_{ul} < 0`: the line amplifies instead
of absorbing. The code keeps the negative value, as for the bound-free
continuum, because the slab radiance
:math:`-(j/\kappa)\,\mathrm{expm1}(-\kappa D)` holds for either sign
(:doc:`/theory/transport`). Boltzmann populations never invert; inverted
pairs can occur only with ``cellDistribution``, from the DSMC sampling.
The emission is clipped at zero (it is non-negative for any physical
population).

Lumped levels and the ratio :math:`g^{\mathrm{line}}/g`
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The factors :math:`g_u^{\mathrm{line}}/g(u)` and
:math:`g_u^{\mathrm{line}}/g(l)` allow a level list in which fine-structure
levels are lumped into one level while the lines stay resolved: the line
then sees the share :math:`g_u^{\mathrm{line}}/g(u)` of the lumped level's
states. In the shipped files every level is J-resolved and the line
weights equal the level weights (checked on all four atomic files), so
the ratios are one and the forms reduce to those of
:doc:`/theory/foundations`.

Multiplets
~~~~~~~~~~

A multiplet is the group of lines between the fine-structure components
:math:`J` of two terms, e.g. the N I 3s :sup:`4`\ P -- 3p
:sup:`4`\ D\ :sup:`o` multiplet near 868 nm used by the ``singleCell_N``
tutorial. ``atomicLines`` computes every component as its own line; there
is no multiplet lumping. The three strongest components in that tutorial
window, as they appear in ``database/atoms/N_I.dat``, are:

     - levels (l, u)
     - :math:`g_l`
     - :math:`g_u`
     - :math:`A_{ul}` [s\ :sup:`-1`]
     - :math:`w_S` [Å]
   * - 8682.666
     - 8, 18
     - 6
     - 8
     - :math:`2.53\times10^7`
     - 0.045
   * - 8685.788
     - 7, 17
     - 4
     - 6
     - :math:`1.88\times10^7`
     - 0.045
   * - 8688.535

     - :math:`1.15\times10^7`

The same Stark width for the three components reflects its source:
Johnston (2006) gives one width per multiplet for the 0-6 eV lines (his
Tables A-2, A-4) and assumes it for every :math:`J` component. Multiplet
tables also exist on the dsmcFoam side of the coupling (radiative decay in
the DSMC ladders); they are derived from the same atomic files and live in
the DSMC_Rovibronic repository.

Which lines are computed
------------------------

For each cell the species is skipped if :math:`n_s < 10` m\ :sup:`-3` or
:math:`T_{\mathrm{trans}} < 10` K, or if the population model returns no
population. Then, line by line:

1. A line whose upper level lies above the bound limit,
   :math:`E_u > E_b` (the lowered ionisation limit returned by the
   population model), is skipped: the upper level is not a bound state.
2. A line farther than :math:`100\max(w_L, w_D) + \Delta\lambda` from the
   grid is skipped (:math:`w_L`, :math:`w_D` the Lorentz and Doppler
   widths below).
3. Otherwise the profile is placed on the grid points of its footprint,
   :math:`\pm 25` Voigt widths, each point receiving the profile integral
   over its own bin (:doc:`/theory/line_shapes`).

Note that the bound limit is :math:`E_b = E_{\mathrm{ion}} - \Delta
E_{\mathrm{ion}}` with :math:`E_{\mathrm{ion}}` the entry
``ionisationEnergy``. A species given ``ionisationEnergy 0`` has no bound
limit (:math:`E_b` = ``GREAT``): every level is kept and every line is
computed, so the entry switches the level cut off. Give the ionisation
energy of the species for physical results; until 2026-11-16 the value 0
made :math:`E_b \le 0` and suppressed every line.

Line widths
-----------

The profile of each line is a Voigt profile built from a Gaussian
(Doppler) and a Lorentzian width. ``atomicLines`` computes the widths (full
widths at half maximum, m); the profile, its Voigt width and its placement
are described in :doc:`/theory/line_shapes`. With :math:`\lambda_0` in m,
:math:`n` the emitter density, :math:`T_{\mathrm{tr}}` its translational
temperature and :math:`m` its mass:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 18 52 30

   * - Width
     - Expression
     - Source (as cited in the code)
   * - Stark
     - :math:`2\,n_e\,w_S\,(T_e/10^4)^{n_S}\times10^{-32}`
     - Johnston (2006) Eq. 3.13
   * - van der Waals
     - :math:`20\,(\lambda_0^2/c)\,4.5214\times10^{-18}\,n_{\mathrm{tot}}
       (3k_B T_{\mathrm{tr}} n_{\mathrm{tot}}/\rho)^{0.3}`
     - form of the verified version
   * - natural
     - :math:`\dfrac{\lambda_0^2}{2\pi c}(\Gamma_u + \Gamma_l)`
     - Laux (1993) Eq. 4.19
   * - phase (impact)
     - :math:`\dfrac{\lambda_0^2}{\pi c}\,\dfrac{\nu_{\mathrm{coll}}}{n}`
     - impact theory
   * - resonance
     - :math:`\dfrac{3}{16\pi^3 c}\sqrt{g_u/g_l}\,\lambda_0^5 A_{ul}\,
       \dfrac{g_l}{g(l)}\,n(l)`
     - Griem (1964) via Laux (1993) Eq. 4.20
   * - Doppler
     - :math:`\sqrt{8k_B\ln 2\,T_{\mathrm{tr}}/m}\;\lambda_0/c`
     - (``ln 2`` coded as 0.69315)

with :math:`w_L` = Stark + van der Waals + natural + phase + resonance.
In the Stark width, :math:`n_e` [m\ :sup:`-3`] is the cell electron
density, :math:`T_e` the cell electron temperature, or the temperature of
the population model when the cell gives none, and :math:`n_S` the entry
``starkExponent`` (1/3 for most N and O lines, Park 1982 as quoted by
Johnston 2006); :math:`n_e = 0` gives no Stark broadening. The natural
width uses the decay rate of each level,

.. math::

   \Gamma_x = \sum_{\text{lines leaving } x}
              \frac{g_u^{\mathrm{line}}}{g(x)}\,A,

the mean decay rate per state of a (possibly lumped) level. The phase
width uses the hard-sphere collision rate of the emitter with every
radiating species :math:`j` of the cell,

.. math::

   \nu_{\mathrm{coll}} = \sum_j 2\,n\,n_j\,\pi(r + r_j)^2
     \sqrt{\frac{2 k_B T_m (m + m_j)}{\pi m m_j}},

with :math:`r` the entry ``radius`` and :math:`T_m` the mass-density
weighted mean translational temperature of the pair. Only the radiating
species of the case are perturbers: a species of the mixture without a
radiator does not broaden the lines.
