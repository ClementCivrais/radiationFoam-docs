Level populations
=================

Every emission and absorption coefficient is proportional to the number
density of the upper or lower level of a transition
(:doc:`/theory/foundations`). The population model provides these number
densities, cell by cell, for every level a radiator needs. radiationFoam
accepts two kinds of input, selected by the top-level entry
``populationModel`` of ``system/radiationProperties``:

* ``boltzmannTemperatures``: the species temperatures of the cell, from
  which the populations are **re-expanded** with an assumed Boltzmann
  shape;
* ``cellDistribution``: the **level distribution** of the cell itself,
  :math:`n(n,v,J)` sampled by dsmcFoam, with no assumption on its shape.

The second route is the purpose of the code: the radiance follows the
state-resolved populations of the gas kinetics directly. The first is the
reference against which it is compared, and the input of codes that only
carry temperatures. Source: ``src/radiation/populations``; classes in
:doc:`/classes/populations`.

The contract: levels, cell state, populations
---------------------------------------------

Three plain-data objects meet in the population model.

* ``radiationLevels`` (``src/radiation/common/radiationLevels``), built
  once by each radiator, lists the levels its lines need: for each level a
  key, a statistical weight :math:`g_i` and an energy :math:`E_i` [J] above
  the species ground level. An atomic level is identified by its label
  (the level index of the atomic file). A diatomic level is identified by
  :math:`(n, v, J)`, the electronic-state label of the molecule file and
  the vibrational and rotational quantum numbers; its energy is also split
  into :math:`E_{\mathrm{el}} + E_{\mathrm{vib}} + E_{\mathrm{rot}}`, and
  the constants of every electronic state are attached (for the partition
  function below).
* ``radiationCellState`` (``src/radiation/common/radiationCellState``)
  holds the state of one cell: per radiating species the number density
  :math:`n_s` and the temperatures ``Ttrans``, ``Telec``, ``Tvib``,
  ``Trot``; the electron density :math:`n_e` and temperature :math:`T_e`;
  and, when given, the level distribution of each species.
* The population model returns :math:`n_i` [m\ :sup:`-3`] of every level
  of the list, plus a **bound limit** :math:`E_b` [J] (atomic lines whose
  upper level lies above :math:`E_b` are not computed) and the electron
  temperature seen by the Stark width.

The radiators never compute populations themselves; they call
``radiationPopulationModel::levelPopulations``. A new population model
(e.g. a collisional-radiative one) is therefore a new derived class, with
no change to the radiators.

Route 1: Boltzmann re-expansion from temperatures
-------------------------------------------------

``boltzmannTemperatures`` assumes that each internal mode of each species
is in a Boltzmann distribution at a temperature of its own. These
temperatures are inputs; the model does not compute them.

Which temperatures
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The cell state carries, per species,

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 18 82

   * - Cell-state entry
     - Used for
   * - ``Telec``
     - atoms: the Boltzmann distribution over the electronic levels;
       diatomics: the distribution over the electronic states
   * - ``Tvib``
     - diatomics: the distribution over :math:`v` within each electronic
       state
   * - ``Trot``
     - diatomics: the distribution over :math:`J`
   * - ``Ttrans``
     - not a population input; Doppler and pressure widths
       (:doc:`/theory/line_shapes`)

Where they come from depends on the transport mode. In ``singleCell`` they
are the entries ``electronicTemperature``, ``vibrationalTemperature``,
``rotationalTemperature`` and ``translationalTemperature`` of each species
in ``cellState``. In ``shockTube1D`` with a macro file they are the
columns ``Telec_<s>``, ``Tvib_<s>``, ``Trot_<s>``, ``Ttrans_<s>``. From a
reconstructed dsmcFoam case they are fields: by default
``translationalT_<s>`` for the translational temperature and
``internalT_<s>`` for the three internal ones. dsmcFoam writes no separate
rotational, vibrational or electronic temperature, because its internal
state is rovibronic: ``internalT_<s>`` is the single temperature whose
Boltzmann distribution over the :math:`(n,v,J)` ladder has the sampled
mean internal energy (``src/radiation/profiles/shockTubeProfile``). Any of
the five can be mapped to another field per species.

Atoms
~~~~~

With the electron temperature :math:`T_e` and the electron density
:math:`n_e`, the ionisation limit is first lowered by the Debye-Hückel
term

.. math::

   \Delta E_{\mathrm{ion}} = 2.9\times10^{-8}\,
     \sqrt{\frac{n_e\,[\mathrm{cm^{-3}}]}{T_e}}\;e
     \quad [\mathrm{J}],

(:math:`n_e` in m\ :sup:`-3` divided by :math:`10^6`), which gives the
bound limit

.. math::

   E_b = E_{\mathrm{ion}} - \Delta E_{\mathrm{ion}},

with :math:`E_{\mathrm{ion}}` the entry ``ionisationEnergy`` of the
species (cm\ :sup:`-1` in the dictionary). :math:`T_e` is the electron
temperature of the cell; when the cell state gives none (:math:`T_e \le 0`)
the electronic temperature :math:`T_{\mathrm{el}}` of the species is used,
and when neither is positive there is no lowering. Both population models
apply this one rule (``radiationPopulationModel::electronTemperature`` and
``boundLimit``), and the Stark width uses the same temperature. A species
without an ionisation energy (``ionisationEnergy`` :math:`\le 0`, e.g. every
diatomic radiator) has no bound limit at all. For example, at
:math:`n_e = 10^{21}` m\ :sup:`-3` and :math:`T_e = 10^4` K the lowering
is :math:`9.2\times10^{-3}` eV.

.. note::

   Until 2026-11-16 ``boltzmannTemperatures`` evaluated the lowering with
   :math:`\max(1\,\mathrm{K}, T_{\mathrm{el}})`: a cold species
   (:math:`T_{\mathrm{el}} \le 0`) in a gas with electrons got a lowering
   at 1 K (0.92 eV at :math:`n_e = 10^{21}` m\ :sup:`-3`), which removed
   the ground-state absorption lines to the levels within 0.92 eV of the
   limit (``verification/library/repro_docsweep.py``, case D2).

The **level cut** keeps the levels of the file up to the last one, in file
order, with :math:`E_i < E_b`:

.. math::

   N_b = \max\{\,i : E_i < E_b\,\}.

The atomic files are sorted by energy, so this is the set of levels below
:math:`E_b`. The cut applies also when :math:`n_e = 0`: then
:math:`\Delta E_{\mathrm{ion}} = 0` and :math:`E_b = E_{\mathrm{ion}}`,
so the autoionising levels at or above the ionisation limit, which the
NIST ASD files list (Kramida et al., 2024; 55 of the 366 N I levels, 346 of
the 580 O I levels), are
never bound and never populated. Only a species with no ionisation energy
(:math:`E_{\mathrm{ion}} \le 0`) keeps every level. If the lowering
reaches the ground level, no level is bound and the species does not
radiate in that cell.

The populations are the Boltzmann distribution over the kept levels:

.. math::

   Q = \sum_{i \le N_b} g_i\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{E_i}{k_B T_{\mathrm{el}}}\right),

.. math::

   n_i = n_s\,\frac{g_i\,\exp(-E_i/k_B T_{\mathrm{el}})}{Q}
   \quad (i \le N_b),
   \qquad n_i = 0 \quad (i > N_b).

:math:`Q` is the truncated sum over the levels of the data file, with no
asymptotic hydrogenic tail. The cut matters at high temperature: without
it the autoionising levels would hold 0.9 % of the O I atoms at 20 000 K
and 4.7 % at 25 000 K (figure below, panel b).

:math:`T_{\mathrm{el}} \le 0` means no electronic excitation (e.g. a DSMC
species with every particle in its ground level). The model then takes the
limit :math:`T_{\mathrm{el}} \to 0`: all atoms in the lowest kept level(s),
split by :math:`g`. A cold atom therefore still absorbs from its ground
level.

.. figure:: /_static/figures/populations/atomic_boltzmann.png
   :width: 100%

   Boltzmann populations of the atomic files as ``boltzmannTemperatures``
   forms them. (a) Population per state :math:`n_i/(n g_i)` of the N I
   levels kept below :math:`E_{\mathrm{ion}}` at
   :math:`T_{\mathrm{el}} = 10\,000` K and :math:`n_e = 0`. (b) Share of
   the atoms the levels at or above :math:`E_{\mathrm{ion}}` would hold if
   they were not cut. Script:
   ``_static/figures/populations/plot_atomic_boltzmann.py``.

Diatomics
~~~~~~~~~

For a diatomic the population is separable: one temperature per mode,

.. math::

   n_i = \frac{n_s\,g_i}{Q}\,
         \exp\!\left[-\frac{1}{k_B}\left(
           \frac{E_{\mathrm{el},i}}{T_{\mathrm{el}}}
         + \frac{E_{\mathrm{vib},i}}{T_{\mathrm{vib}}}
         + \frac{E_{\mathrm{rot},i}}{T_{\mathrm{rot}}}\right)\right],

with the energy parts given by ``diatomicBands`` for each level
:math:`(s, v, J)`:

.. math::

   E_{\mathrm{el}} = T_{e,s}, \qquad
   E_{\mathrm{vib}} = G_s(v), \qquad
   E_{\mathrm{rot}} = B_s(v)\,J(J+1),

.. math::

   G_s(v) = \omega_e x - \omega_e x_e x^2 + \omega_e y_e x^3
          + \omega_e z_e x^4,
   \qquad
   B_s(v) = B_e - \alpha_e x,
   \qquad x = v + \tfrac12.

:math:`G_s(v)` includes the zero-point energy, and :math:`g_i` is the
weight of the level (:doc:`/theory/diatomic_bands`). The rotational energy
of the populations is :math:`B_v J(J+1)` only (no centrifugal or
spin-splitting terms, which the line positions do include).

The partition function is summed over **all** electronic states of the
molecule file, with the rotational sum in closed form:

.. math::

   Q = \sum_s g_{e,s}\,
       \exp\!\left[\min\!\left(700, -\frac{T_{e,s}}{k_B T_{\mathrm{el}}}\right)\right]
       \sum_{v=0,1,\dots}
       \exp\!\left(-\frac{G_s(v)}{k_B T_{\mathrm{vib}}}\right)
       \frac{k_B T_{\mathrm{rot}}}{B_s(v)}
       \left[1 - \exp\!\left(\frac{G_s(v) - D_{0,s}}{k_B T_{\mathrm{rot}}}\right)\right].

The :math:`v`-sum stops at the first :math:`v` with :math:`G > D_0` or
:math:`G` below the previous :math:`G`, and a term counts only if
:math:`B_s(v) > 0`. The last factor truncates the rotational sum at the
dissociation energy :math:`D_{0,s}`. There is no symmetry number: the
homonuclear spin statistics enter through the alternation factor of each
line, whose mean is one (:doc:`/theory/diatomic_bands`). The molecule
files hold only the states of their bands plus the ground state, so
:math:`Q` omits the other electronic states, a small effect below about
10 000 K (``src/radiation/database/README.md``).

Limits and checks of the diatomic re-expansion:

* :math:`T_{\mathrm{el}} \le 0`: only the lowest electronic state(s) are
  populated (the :math:`T_{\mathrm{el}} \to 0` limit);
* :math:`T_{\mathrm{vib}} < 10` K: no population; the species neither
  emits nor absorbs in that cell, with a warning once per species (the
  separable distribution has no :math:`T_{\mathrm{vib}} \to 0` limit,
  because the zero-point energies are inside :math:`G(v)`);
* :math:`T_{\mathrm{rot}} \le 0`: fatal error;
* no bound limit (:math:`E_b` is set to a very large number).

Lower population seen by the absorption
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The absorption of a line needs :math:`n_l/g_l`. By default
(``radiationPopulationModel::lineLowerPopulation``) this is the lower
level's own population divided by its weight. ``boltzmannTemperatures``
overrides it for diatomics with the form of the verified version: the
upper population times the ratio built from the line's own term-value
differences,

.. math::

   \frac{n_l}{g_l} = \frac{n_u}{g_u}\,
     \exp\!\left(\frac{\Delta E_{\mathrm{el}}}{k_B T_{\mathrm{el}}}
               + \frac{\Delta E_{\mathrm{vib}}}{k_B T_{\mathrm{vib}}}
               + \frac{\Delta F}{k_B T_{\mathrm{rot}}}\right),

where :math:`\Delta E_{\mathrm{el}} = T_e' - T_e''`,
:math:`\Delta E_{\mathrm{vib}} = G' - G''` and
:math:`\Delta F = F' - F''` is the rotational term difference of the line
**including** the centrifugal and spin-splitting terms. It therefore
differs slightly from the lower level's own Boltzmann population at high
:math:`J`. ``cellDistribution`` knows levels only and uses
:math:`n_l/g_l` of the level list.

Route 2: the cell's level distribution
--------------------------------------

``cellDistribution`` takes the populations from the distribution of the
cell state, i.e. from the :math:`n(n,v,J)` sampled by dsmcFoam. No shape
is assumed: a Treanor-like vibrational tail, an overpopulated metastable
or an inverted pair of levels is carried into the spectrum as it is.

Distribution file
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The distribution of one cell is an OpenFOAM dictionary with one
sub-dictionary per species (``radiationCellState::readDistribution``). It
is written by the dsmcFoam function object ``dsmcRadiationDistribution``
(DSMC_Rovibronic repository, ``src/lagrangian/DSMC/radiationCoupling``),
one file ``cell<k>`` per selected cell plus an index file ``cells``:

.. code-block:: cpp

   cell 0;                         // cell label, used in the diagnostics
   N
   {
       numberDensity   9.6e20;     // all levels of the species (not read)
       levels
       (
           (1)        9.5e20       // atom: one level label
           (6 7 8)    3.1e15       // atom: a group of level labels
       );
       g ( 4 12 );                 // optional: weight of the DSMC level
       coverage complete;          // optional (atoms)
   }
   N2
   {
       levels
       (
           (X1Sigma 0 12)  1.2e18  // diatomic: (electronic state, v, J)
       );
       coverage ( (X1Sigma 0 0 98) (X1Sigma 1 0 97) );   // optional
   }

Each entry of ``levels`` is a list of identifiers and a number density
[m\ :sup:`-3`]. Identifiers are words, integers or quoted strings (for a
state name that is not a valid word, e.g. ``"1c1Pi"``). An atomic entry
lists level labels of the atomic file (the dsmcFoam writer reads them from
a level map of the DSMC ladder onto the radiation file); a diatomic entry
is exactly (electronic-state label of the molecule file, :math:`v`,
:math:`J`). A species with an empty list ``levels ( );`` is present with
no populated level; a species absent from the file has no distribution,
which is a fatal error for a radiating species. Entries the reader ignores
(``numberDensity``, ``parcelSamples``, ``parcelCounts``, ``energy``) may be
present.

The file is named by ``cellState { distributionFile ...; }`` in
``singleCell``, and by the directory of ``shockTube1DCoeffs/distribution``
in ``shockTube1D`` (each distribution cell is matched to the nearest
profile cell, which gives :math:`T_{\mathrm{trans}}`, :math:`n_s` and the
electrons for the line shapes); see :doc:`/theory/transport`.

Mapping and normalisation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

An entry with number density :math:`N` covers every spectroscopic level
whose key it lists: for an atom the levels with the given labels, for a
diatomic every level with key "state v J", i.e. all the fine-structure
components of the data that share :math:`(n,v,J)`. :math:`N` is split over
these levels by statistical weight,

.. math::

   n_i = N\,\frac{g_i}{\sum_{j\in\mathrm{entry}} g_j},

so the levels of a degenerate group are equally populated per state
(:math:`n_i = N` for a single level). When the file gives the weight
:math:`g_D` of the DSMC level of the entry (list ``g``) and
:math:`g_D > \sum_j g_j`, the DSMC level holds states with no
spectroscopic level, as for the complete hydrogenic Rydberg shells of the
DSMC atomic ladders (e.g. N I DSMC levels with 450 to 1800 states, of which
the NIST file lists 162 to 80). The split is then

.. math::

   n_i = N\,\frac{g_i}{g_D},

the same population per state over the whole DSMC level; the number
density left on states without a spectroscopic level is reported in the
log and does not radiate.

The populations are **not renormalised** to the species number density.
The log (once per species, at the first cell) gives the sum of the
populations against :math:`n_s` of the cell state, the levels covered by
no entry (count and keys), and the diatomic entries that lie outside the
level set of the data: an entry of a known electronic state whose
:math:`(v,J)` is not among the levels of any line (the DSMC ladders hold
:math:`J = 0, \dots, J_{\max}` and more :math:`v` than the line lists) is
skipped and counted. Any other entry that matches no level, and a level
covered by two entries, are fatal errors.

Bound limit
~~~~~~~~~~~

The distribution has no electronic temperature. The bound limit uses the
Debye-Hückel lowering above with the **cell electron temperature**
:math:`T_e`, or the species :math:`T_{\mathrm{el}}` of the cell state when
the cell gives no :math:`T_e` (:math:`T_e \le 0`), and no lowering if
neither is positive. The Stark width uses the same temperature. With
:math:`T_e = T_{\mathrm{el}}` the bound limit and the widths of
``boltzmannTemperatures`` are recovered exactly.

Missing levels: ``zero`` or ``boltzmannFill``
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The DSMC ladder may not carry every level the spectroscopic data list
(Rydberg levels of atoms, electronic states absent from the ladder, the
level selection of the writer). The entry ``missingLevels`` of
``cellDistributionCoeffs`` decides what these levels get:

* ``zero`` (default): :math:`n_i = 0`;
* ``boltzmannFill``: the Boltzmann population at the electronic
  temperature of the species in the cell state,

  .. math::

     n_i = n_s\,\frac{g_i\,\exp(-E_i/k_B T_{\mathrm{el}})}{Q(T_{\mathrm{el}})},

  with :math:`T_{\mathrm{el}} \ge 10` K required (fatal error otherwise).
  For an atom, :math:`Q` sums over **all** levels of the file below the
  bound limit (the cut of ``boltzmannTemperatures``); levels above the cut
  are not filled. For a diatomic, :math:`E_i = E_{\mathrm{el}} +
  E_{\mathrm{vib}} + E_{\mathrm{rot}}` and :math:`Q` is the partition
  function of ``boltzmannTemperatures`` with
  :math:`T_{\mathrm{el}} = T_{\mathrm{vib}} = T_{\mathrm{rot}}`, i.e. the
  rovibronic Boltzmann distribution at one temperature. From a dsmcFoam
  case, :math:`T_{\mathrm{el}}` is ``internalT_<s>``.

Sampled levels keep their populations untouched; the filled populations
are not renormalised either (their sum is reported).

The ``coverage`` entry separates a level the DSMC ladder does **not**
carry from a level it carries but that received no parcel in the
averaging block. Only the first kind is filled. Filling the second kind
would bias rarely sampled levels upwards: for a Poisson count of mean
:math:`\lambda` per block, the estimator would become
:math:`E[\hat n] = n_{\mathrm{true}} + e^{-\lambda}\,n_{\mathrm{fill}}`.
For atoms, ``coverage complete;`` means the list has one entry for every
carried level, with zero density for unsampled ones. For diatomics, the
coverage is a list of runs (state, :math:`v`, :math:`J_0`, :math:`J_1`)
of carried levels. A file without ``coverage`` (written before
2026-11-14) fills every absent level, with a warning once per species.

Diagnostics of the fill
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

With ``boltzmannFill`` every call of a radiator (species, cell, window)
appends a row to the CSV file ``fillReport`` (default
``<case>/postProcessing/radiationFoam/cellDistributionFill.csv``):

.. code-block:: text

   cell,species,lambdaMin_nm,lambdaMax_nm,levels,sampled_entries,filled_levels,
   filled_population_fraction,j_filled,j_total,filled_emission_fraction

``filled_population_fraction`` is the filled population over :math:`n_s`;
``j_filled`` and ``j_total`` [W m\ :sup:`-3` sr\ :sup:`-1`] are
:math:`\int j_\lambda\,d\lambda` over the window of the lines whose upper
level is filled and of all lines; their ratio
``filled_emission_fraction`` is the share of the species' emission (and of
its optically thin radiance) that comes from modelled rather than sampled
levels. Filled lower levels also change the absorption, which this ratio
does not count. At the end of the run the log gives, per species and
window, the number of rows, the range of filled levels, the aggregate
filled share and its maximum.

Which levels emit: line selection
---------------------------------

For a diatomic line, ``boltzmannTemperatures`` places a line only if its
emission passes the threshold of ``diatomicBands`` (the absorption follows
the emission). ``cellDistribution`` takes the populations as given: a line
whose upper level is empty or unsampled is still placed for its
absorption, when that passes the same threshold. A level that DSMC did not
populate thus still absorbs from its lower level
(:doc:`/theory/diatomic_bands`).
