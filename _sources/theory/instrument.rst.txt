Instrument model: reproducing a shock-tube measurement
======================================================

Source: ``src/radiation/instrument/`` (``instrumentFunctions/`` and
``shockTubeInstrument/``), used by the ``shockTube1D`` transport mode
(``src/radiation/transport/derived/shockTube1D/shockTube1D.C``) and verified
by ``applications/test/radiationInstrument``.

The transport modes of radiationFoam compute the radiance
:math:`I(x,\lambda)` that leaves the gas (:doc:`/theory/transport`). A
shock-tube spectrometer such as the NASA Electric Arc Shock Tube (EAST) does
not record that quantity directly. It records it blurred in wavelength by the
spectrometer, blurred along the tube by the optics, the camera and the motion
of the shock during the exposure, and integrated over the wavelength windows
of its figures. This page explains each of these steps, the way the code
evaluates them, and the units of every number that is compared with EAST.
The measurement model follows the definitions of Brandis and Cruden (2017),
Sec. III.B, IV and Apps. C-D, and Cruden and Brandis (2020), App. A.


What the spectrometer sees
--------------------------

In EAST the shock travels down a tube of diameter :math:`D` = 10.16 cm. An
imaging spectrometer looks across the tube, through its centreline, and
records the radiance along the line of sight on a two-dimensional detector:
one axis is the wavelength :math:`\lambda`, the other the position :math:`x`
along the tube. ``shockTube1D`` computes the same line-of-sight radiance cell
by cell: each cell of the 1-D profile is a uniform slab of thickness
:math:`D`, and its radiance is :math:`I(x,\lambda)` in
W m\ :sup:`-2` sr\ :sup:`-1` m\ :sup:`-1` (see :doc:`/theory/transport`).

Three things separate :math:`I(x,\lambda)` from the recorded signal:

#. the **instrument line shape** (ILS): a monochromatic line is recorded with
   a finite width set by the slit, the grating and the detector;
#. the **spatial resolution function** (SRF): a point source along the tube
   is recorded with a finite extent set by the optics, the camera and the
   distance the shock moves while the camera gate is open;
#. the **reduction** that EAST applies to its data: band (window) integrals,
   the location of the shock front, the nonequilibrium metric and the
   spectra at chosen distances behind the front.

The first two are convolutions, the third is post-processing of the convolved
field.


Convolution with a normalised kernel
------------------------------------

Every instrument function is a normalised kernel :math:`K(s)` of the offset
:math:`s` between the measured coordinate :math:`y` and the source coordinate
:math:`y'` (:math:`y` is either :math:`\lambda` or :math:`x`):

.. math::

   I_\mathrm{meas}(y) = \int K(y - y')\, I_\mathrm{true}(y')\, \mathrm{d}y',
   \qquad
   \int K(s)\,\mathrm{d}s = 1 .

:math:`K(s)` [1/length] is the signal recorded at :math:`y` from a unit delta
source at :math:`y'`, i.e. the way an ILS is measured with a calibration lamp
line. Brandis and Cruden (2017, App. D) write the SRF as
:math:`I_\mathrm{meas}(x) = \int \mathrm{SRF}(\delta x)\, I_\mathrm{true}(x +
\delta x)\, \mathrm{d}\delta x`, i.e. :math:`K(s) = \mathrm{SRF}(-s)`; every
EAST function is symmetric, so the two forms agree. The unit area means that a
convolution moves energy between wavelengths (or positions) but does not
create or destroy it.

The full measured field is the double convolution

.. math::

   I_\mathrm{meas}(x,\lambda) = \iint K_x(x - x')\, K_\lambda(\lambda - \lambda')\,
   I(x',\lambda')\, \mathrm{d}x'\, \mathrm{d}\lambda' .

The two kernels act on different axes, so they commute.
``shockTubeInstrument::measure`` applies the ILS to the spectrum of every cell
first, then the SRF to the profile of every wavelength. Either kernel may be
absent, in which case it is the identity.

Each kernel class provides its cumulative distribution

.. math::

   C(s) = \int_{-\infty}^{s} K(t)\,\mathrm{d}t ,

its density :math:`K(s)` (``pdf``), the half range :math:`R` beyond which
:math:`K = 0` (``support``) and a characteristic full width (``width``: a FWHM
or a base width). Analytic kernels give :math:`C(s)` in closed form. The others
fill a piecewise-linear table of nodes :math:`t_k` and densities :math:`p_k`;
between nodes :math:`C` is then the exact integral of the linear density,

.. math::

   C(t) = C_k + p_k\,\tau + \frac{(p_{k+1} - p_k)\,\tau^2}{2 h_k},
   \qquad \tau = t - t_k,\quad h_k = t_{k+1} - t_k ,

normalised so that :math:`C(t_\mathrm{last}) = 1` (numerical normalisation,
as the EAST ILS requires: Brandis and Cruden 2017, App. C).


Discrete convolution on the computational grid
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The radiance is known at grid points :math:`y_0 < y_1 < \dots < y_{N-1}`
(uniform in wavelength, possibly non-uniform in :math:`x` for a DSMC mesh).
``instrumentFunction::convolve`` treats each point as the owner of a bin:

.. math::

   e_0 = y_0,\quad e_N = y_{N-1},\quad e_i = \tfrac12 (y_{i-1} + y_i),
   \qquad \Delta_i = e_{i+1} - e_i ,

so :math:`\Delta_i` is the trapezoid weight of point :math:`i`. Source
:math:`j` carries the content :math:`c_j = \Delta_j f_j`, and the output is
the bin average of the kernels placed on the sources:

.. math::

   g_i = \sum_j \frac{c_j}{\Delta_i\, n_j}
   \Big[ C(e_{i+1} - y_j) - C(e_i - y_j) \Big],
   \qquad
   n_j = C(e_N - y_j) - C(e_0 - y_j) .

Using the bin mass :math:`C(e_{i+1}-y_j) - C(e_i-y_j)` instead of a sampled
:math:`K(y_i - y_j)` has three consequences:

* a kernel narrower than the bins reduces to the identity (no aliasing of a
  narrow top hat or Gaussian);
* a delta source on the grid returns the bin-averaged kernel exactly;
* with ``conserveAtEdges true`` (the default) the factor :math:`n_j`
  gives back to the window the part of a kernel that falls outside it, so the
  trapezoid integral :math:`\sum_i \Delta_i g_i = \sum_j \Delta_j f_j` holds
  to round-off for any grid and any kernel. With ``conserveAtEdges false``,
  :math:`n_j = 1` and the content scattered beyond the window edges is lost,
  as on a detector of finite size.

The cost is :math:`O(NM)` with :math:`M` the number of points within the
kernel support. On a uniform grid (every spacing within :math:`10^{-10}` of
the mean, relative) the interior weights depend on :math:`i - j` only and are
computed once per call (:math:`2M+2` evaluations of :math:`C`). On any other
grid the :math:`NM` weights and the :math:`n_j` are computed once and stored with
the grid, and reused while the following calls pass the same grid (the spatial
convolution: one call per wavelength on the same cell centres); the result is
the same bit for bit (store capped at :math:`2^{25}` weights). A kernel that
spans the window on a uniform grid (stencil :math:`2M+1 > N`, :math:`N \ge
4096`; the EAST line shape over the detector) makes the sum a linear
convolution of the amplitudes :math:`c_j/n_j` with the interior weights
:math:`w(i-j)`: it is done by FFT (radix 2, length :math:`L \ge N + 2M`,
:math:`O(L \log L)` instead of :math:`O(N^2)`), :math:`n_j` from prefix sums of the
same weights and the two half bins at the grid ends with their exact weights;
the result differs from the direct sum by round-off only (at most
:math:`5\times 10^{-14}` relative on the 57-16 VUV spectrum; option
``convolution``).

.. tip::

   Because the convolution conserves the integral, a band integral taken over
   a range much wider than the ILS is insensitive to the ILS. Peak spectral
   radiances and the peaks of the spatial profiles are not.


Instrument line shape (spectral kernels)
----------------------------------------

The available kernels are listed below; their dictionary keywords are in
:doc:`/classes/instrument`. Widths are in metres unless ``lengthUnit``
(``m``, ``cm``, ``mm``, ``um``, ``nm``) says otherwise.

**Gaussian** (``gaussian``), given its FWHM or its standard deviation:

.. math::

   K(s) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma}\exp\!\left(-\frac{s^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),
   \quad \sigma = \frac{\mathrm{FWHM}}{2\sqrt{2\ln 2}},
   \quad C(s) = \tfrac12\Big[1 + \mathrm{erf}\big(s/(\sqrt2\,\sigma)\big)\Big],

cut at :math:`|s| = 10\sigma` by default (``cutoff``, a truncated mass of
about :math:`1.5\times10^{-23}`). It is also the EAST infrared camera
function of Brandis and Cruden (2017, App. D) with FWHM :math:`= 2 w_G`.

**Voigt** (``voigt``), from the FWHM :math:`f_G` of its Gaussian part and
:math:`f_L` of its Lorentzian part. With ``form exact`` (default) the
convolution :math:`G \otimes L` is tabulated at start-up on uniform nodes of
spacing :math:`f_V/\mathrm{resolution}` (default 100 nodes per
:math:`f_V`), the Lorentzian averaged over a node cell and convolved with the
Gaussian cell masses (error :math:`O(h^2/\sigma^2)`). With
``form pseudoVoigt`` the kernel is the Thompson, Cox and Hastings (1987)
pseudo-Voigt with an analytic CDF:

.. math::

   f &= \big(f_G^5 + 2.69269 f_G^4 f_L + 2.42843 f_G^3 f_L^2
          + 4.47163 f_G^2 f_L^3 + 0.07842 f_G f_L^4 + f_L^5\big)^{1/5},\\
   \eta &= 1.36603\, r - 0.47719\, r^2 + 0.11116\, r^3,
   \qquad r = f_L/f,\\
   K(s) &= \eta\, L(s; f) + (1 - \eta)\, G(s; f).

The Lorentzian wings are cut at :math:`|s| = 100 f_V` by default and the
kernel renormalised there, with :math:`f_V` the Olivero and Longbothum (1977)
width

.. math::

   f_V = 0.5346\, f_L + \sqrt{0.2166\, f_L^2 + f_G^2} .

:math:`f_G = 0` gives a Lorentzian, :math:`f_L = 0` a Gaussian.

**EAST line shapes** (``eastLineShape``). Brandis and Cruden (2017,
Sec. IV.A and App. C) fit the measured EAST line shapes with two empirical
forms, parameterised by the Gaussian and Lorentzian *half* widths at half
maximum :math:`w_G`, :math:`w_L` of their tables (published in nm: set
``lengthUnit nm``). With

.. math::

   G(s) = \frac{1}{w_G}\sqrt{\frac{\ln 2}{\pi}}
          \exp\!\left[-\ln 2\,\Big(\frac{s}{w_G}\Big)^2\right],
   \qquad
   L(s) = \frac{1}{\pi w_L}\,\frac{1}{(s/w_L)^2 + 1},
   \qquad V = L \otimes G ,

the two forms are

.. math::

   \mathrm{ILS1}(s) &= \frac{V(s)^{1/2}}{\int_{-R}^{R} V(t)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}t}
   \qquad (\texttt{form sqrtVoigt}),\\
   \mathrm{ILS2}(s) &= \frac{G(s) + a\, L(s)}{1 + a},
   \quad 0 \le a \le 1
   \qquad (\texttt{form gaussLorentz}).

ILS1 is used below 500 nm and for the camera part of the spatial function;
ILS2 for the longer-wavelength cameras. The square root of a Voigt decays as
:math:`1/|s|`, so its integral diverges: the normalisation needs a finite half
range :math:`R` (``cutoff``, mandatory). Brandis and Cruden (2017, App. C)
write the normalisation integral to infinity, state that it "is not expressed
analytically, and must be done numerically" and give no range. The database
applies both forms over the detector (since 2026-11-17): the window is the
camera range (``windowMargin 0``), :math:`R` is the width of the camera range,
so that the kernel of every source spans the detector, and every kernel is
normalised over it (``conserveAtEdges``). The long-range wings are produced
by the intensified CCD (Brandis et al. 2015, p. 213; "the profile of
radiation falling on the CCD coupled with the charge spread function of the
CCD", Brandis and Cruden 2017, Sec. IV.A), which spreads only the light that
falls on it, and the functions are "normalized to conserve the area under the
curve" (ibid.): the integral of the measured spectrum over the camera range
equals that of the source spectrum. A band that extends beyond the camera
range is integrated over the overlap (the 170-180 nm window of a camera that
ends at 178 nm). In the EAST 57-16 VUV spectrum the floor between the N I
multiplets at 149.3 and 174.3 nm (159.5-164 nm) is 0.95 of EAST with this rule
and 0.14 with the former :math:`R` = 10 nm (which cut it off at exactly 10 nm
from both multiplets); over ten Test 57 shots the floor follows the line
content of the camera (``docs/reports/EAST_VUV_FLOOR_FIX_20261117.md``; the
former range study ``docs/reports/EAST_ILS_CUTOFF_20261117.md``). ILS2 is
truncated at :math:`R` and renormalised with its analytic CDF.

For a square-root Voigt with zero Lorentz width, the finite-range
density is proportional to :math:`\exp[-\ln(2)s^2/(2w_G^2)]`.
With zero Gaussian width its integrated mass is proportional to
:math:`\operatorname{asinh}(s/w_L)`. The implementation evaluates
these limits analytically on the chosen support.

.. figure:: /_static/figures/theory/instrument_ils.png
   :width: 60%
   :align: center

   Spectral kernels for the EAST UV/Vis camera half widths of shot 50-29
   (:math:`w_G` = 0.28 nm, :math:`w_L` = 0.070 nm), all of unit area:
   sqrt-Voigt ILS1 normalised on :math:`|s| \le 10` nm, Gauss + Lorentz ILS2
   (:math:`a` = 0.2, illustrative), the Voigt and the Gaussian of the same
   half widths. The ILS1 wings decay as :math:`1/|s|`, which is why its peak
   depends on the cut-off. Analytical curves; script
   ``_static/figures/theory/instrument_kernels.py``.

**Tabulated** (``tabulated``): a two-column ASCII file of offset and recorded
response (any scale), e.g. a calibration-lamp line. The density is linear
between the file nodes and normalised to unit area over the file range, so
nothing is resampled. ``centre`` subtracts a given offset;
``centreFirstMoment true`` puts the centroid at zero instead.

**Top hat** and **trapezoid** are mostly spatial kernels (next section), but
any kernel can be used on either axis.


Spatial resolution and the camera gate smear
--------------------------------------------

Brandis and Cruden (2017, App. D) write the EAST spatial resolution function
as the convolution of three parts,

.. math::

   \mathrm{SRF} = f_\mathrm{opt} \otimes f_\mathrm{cam} \otimes f_\mathrm{motion},

built in radiationFoam with a ``composite`` function whose sub-dictionaries
are the three parts:

* :math:`f_\mathrm{opt}`, the optics: a triangle of base :math:`d_\mathrm{opt}`
  (Eq. D1) or a trapezoid of bottom :math:`d_\mathrm{opt,2}` and top
  :math:`d_\mathrm{opt,1}` (Eq. D3), class ``trapezoid`` of bottom width
  :math:`b`, top width :math:`t` and height :math:`2/(b + t)`:

  .. math::

     K(s) = \frac{2}{b+t}\,\min\!\left[1,\;
     \max\!\left(0,\; \frac{b/2 - |s|}{(b - t)/2}\right)\right] ;

* :math:`f_\mathrm{cam}`, the camera: a sqrt-Voigt (``eastLineShape``) or a
  Gaussian (infrared camera), with widths in cm;
* :math:`f_\mathrm{motion}`, the smear of the shock moving during the gate:
  a top hat of width

  .. math::

     d_\mathrm{gate} = U_s\, t_\mathrm{gate},
     \qquad K(s) = \frac{1}{d_\mathrm{gate}}\ \ \text{for}\ |s| < d_\mathrm{gate}/2 ,

  given either as ``width`` or as ``shockSpeed`` [m/s] and ``gateTime`` [s]
  (always SI; Eq. D2). For shot 50-29 the VUV camera smear is 0.514 cm and the
  UV/Vis smear 0.257 cm, far larger than the optics (0.041 cm). The top
  hat is centred: it assumes that :math:`x = 0` is the shock position at
  mid-gate. Referred to the shock position at the gate opening, the smear is
  the same top hat shifted by :math:`d_\mathrm{gate}/2`.

The composite is built once at start-up on a uniform table: nodes
:math:`t_k = -R + k h` with :math:`R` the sum of the component supports and
:math:`h` = (smallest component width)/``resolution`` (default 200), capped at
``maxNodes`` (default 400 001) nodes. The components are folded one by one,

.. math::

   K_\mathrm{new}(t_k) = \frac1h \sum_m a_m
   \Big[C_B\big(t_k - t_m + \tfrac h2\big) - C_B\big(t_k - t_m - \tfrac h2\big)\Big],
   \qquad
   a_m = C_A\big(t_m + \tfrac h2\big) - C_A\big(t_m - \tfrac h2\big),

with :math:`A` the functions folded so far and :math:`B` the next one. Both
factors are cell-integrated, so top hats and kernels narrower than :math:`h`
are handled, and the error is :math:`O(h^2)` of the smallest width.

.. figure:: /_static/figures/theory/instrument_srf.png
   :width: 60%
   :align: center

   Spatial resolution function of the EAST VUV camera of shot 50-29
   (``tutorials/EAST/east5029``): camera sqrt-Voigt (:math:`w_G` = 0.026 cm,

   48.8 cm\ :sup:`-1`). The gate smear sets the width of the composite; the
   camera sets its wings. Analytical curves; script

**Spatial bins.** A DSMC profile may have micrometre cells, far below the
EAST resolution (0.2-5 mm). With ``spatialBinWidth`` :math:`h_b` the raw
radiance of the cells is first averaged over uniform bins
:math:`[x_\mathrm{min} + i h_b,\ x_\mathrm{min} + (i+1) h_b)`, each cell
weighted by its width (midpoint to midpoint) and each bin placed at the
weighted mean :math:`x` of its cells (empty bins are skipped). This bounds the
memory and the cost of the measurement and adds a top hat of width
:math:`h_b` to the SRF.


Band (spectral-window) radiance
-------------------------------

EAST reports most of its results as radiance integrated over a wavelength
window :math:`[\lambda_a, \lambda_b]`:

.. math::

   B(x) = \int_{\lambda_a}^{\lambda_b} I_\mathrm{meas}(x,\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda
   \qquad [\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}] .

``shockTubeInstrument::bandIntegral`` integrates the piecewise-linear
interpolant of the measured spectrum exactly: the band ends are clipped to the
grid, the interpolant is evaluated at the clipped ends and the trapezoid rule
is applied interval by interval. The bands of a camera are given in
``bandUnit`` (``nm`` by default; ``um`` and ``m`` are accepted). A band that
extends beyond the measured range is clipped with a warning.

.. note::

   The *raw* band integrals of ``bands.csv`` (no instrument) use a different
   rule: the sum of :math:`I_i\,\Delta\lambda` over the grid points inside the
   band, a point on a band edge counting as inside
   (:doc:`/guide/post_processing`). The two agree when the band is wide
   compared with the grid spacing.


Shock alignment
---------------

The computed profile has its own coordinate :math:`x`; EAST plots its data
against the distance behind the shock front. Brandis and Cruden (2017) give no
absolute :math:`x = 0`, so both are aligned on the front. The front
:math:`x_0` is found on the measured band profile of one band (``shockFront``
``band``, default the first band) by one of three criteria, or set directly by
``shockFront`` ``position``:

``peakRadiance`` (default)
   The location of the maximum of the profile, refined by the vertex of the
   parabola through the three points around it. With :math:`k` the index of the
   maximum, :math:`a = x_k - x_{k-1}`, :math:`b = x_k - x_{k+1}`,
   :math:`f_a = f_k - f_{k+1}` and :math:`f_b = f_k - f_{k-1}`,

   .. math::

      x_0 = x_k - \frac12\,\frac{a^2 f_a - b^2 f_b}{a f_a - b f_b} .

   This is the EAST definition: "the shock front is defined as the location of
   peak radiance" (Brandis and Cruden 2017, Sec. III.B).

``maxGradient``
   The location of the steepest rise of the profile from the upstream side
   into the shocked gas: the slopes at the interval midpoints, signed so that a
   rise into the shocked gas is positive, then the parabola-refined maximum.
   A steeper *decrease* downstream (end of the test gas, an expansion, the
   domain end) is not taken. For shots without an overshoot EAST takes the
   inflection where the radiance reaches equilibrium; this criterion is the
   discrete counterpart.

``riseFraction``
   The first :math:`x`, scanning from the upstream side, where the profile
   crosses :math:`f_\mathrm{pre} + \phi\,(f_\mathrm{post} - f_\mathrm{pre})`
   (linear interpolation), with :math:`\phi` = ``fraction`` (0.5 by default:
   the 50 % rise). :math:`f_\mathrm{pre}` and :math:`f_\mathrm{post}` are the
   upstream and downstream end values unless ``preValue`` / ``postValue``
   are given.

``upstreamSide low`` (default) means that :math:`x` increases from the free
stream into the shocked gas. The distance behind the shock is then

.. math::

   d = \sigma\,(x - x_0), \qquad \sigma = +1\ (\texttt{low}),\quad -1\ (\texttt{high}).


Nonequilibrium metric and measured spectra
------------------------------------------

The **nonequilibrium metric** of a band is the measured band radiance
integrated over :math:`\pm h` around the front and divided by the tube
diameter (Brandis and Cruden 2017, Sec. III.B; Cruden and Brandis 2020, Eq. 1):

.. math::

   M = \frac{1}{D} \int_{x_0 - h}^{x_0 + h} B(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
   \qquad [\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}],

with :math:`h` = ``metricHalfWidth`` (2 cm in EAST and by default) and
:math:`D` the ``diameter`` of ``shockTube1DCoeffs``. The interval is clipped
to the profile and the clipped range is reported. In optically thick ranges
EAST notes that the metric is not physically meaningful.

**Measured spectra** are extracted from the measured field in three ways:

* ``at d``: the spectrum at distance :math:`d` behind the front, linearly
  interpolated in :math:`x` between the two cells around
  :math:`x_0 + \sigma d`; ``at peak`` takes the parabola-refined peak of the
  front band;
* ``average (d1 d2)``: the mean spectrum over the distance range,
  :math:`\frac{1}{x_b - x_a}\int_{x_a}^{x_b} I_\mathrm{meas}\,\mathrm{d}x`,
  exact for the piecewise-linear profile (EAST "equilibrium" spectra);
* ``metric h``: the spectral nonequilibrium metric
  :math:`\frac1D \int_{x_0-h}^{x_0+h} I_\mathrm{meas}\,\mathrm{d}x`.


Units of the quantities compared with EAST
------------------------------------------

Everything inside the code is SI. EAST publishes spectral radiance in
W cm\ :sup:`-2` sr\ :sup:`-1` µm\ :sup:`-1`, band radiance and metrics in
W cm\ :sup:`-2` sr\ :sup:`-1`, wavelengths in nm and distances in cm. The
conversions are static constants of ``shockTubeInstrument``:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 30 20 20

   * - Quantity
     - Written as
     - Factor from SI
     - Constant
   * - Spectral radiance
     - W cm\ :sup:`-2` sr\ :sup:`-1` µm\ :sup:`-1`
     - :math:`10^{-10}`
     - ``toEASTSpectral``
   * - Band radiance, metric
     - W cm\ :sup:`-2` sr\ :sup:`-1`
     - :math:`10^{-4}`
     - ``toEASTBand``
   * - Wavelength
     - nm
     - :math:`10^{9}`
     - (none)
   * - Distance behind the front
     - ``distanceUnit`` (m, cm, mm)
     - 1, :math:`10^{2}`, :math:`10^{3}`
     - (none)

(:math:`1\ \mathrm{m^{-2}} = 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{cm^{-2}}` and
:math:`1\ \mathrm{m^{-1}} = 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{\mu m^{-1}}`, hence
:math:`10^{-10}` for the spectral radiance.) The ``instrument_<camera>_*.csv``
files are written in these units; the raw outputs and the binary measured
field stay SI (:doc:`/guide/post_processing`).

EAST quotes a calibration uncertainty of about 30 % (Cruden and Brandis 2020),
which is the scale of agreement to expect in an absolute comparison.


Code path
---------

In ``shockTube1D`` (one sub-dictionary of ``instrument`` per camera):

#. at start-up each camera builds its ``shockTubeInstrument`` (and the
   instrument functions, including the composite tables) and selects its
   window and, optionally, a ``wavelengthRange`` of it;
#. in the cell loop the raw radiance of every cell on the camera range is
   copied (or accumulated into its spatial bin); the raw outputs are not
   changed;
#. after the loop the cells are sorted by :math:`x` (two cells at the same
   :math:`x` stop the run), and ``shockTubeInstrument::measure`` applies the
   ILS then the SRF;
#. the band profiles, the front :math:`x_0`, the distances, the metrics and
   the requested spectra are computed and written
   (``instrument_<camera>_bands.csv``, ``instrument_<camera>_spectra.csv``,
   optionally ``instrument_<camera>_field.bin``).

The same classes are driven without the solver by
``Test-radiationInstrument`` (``applications/test/radiationInstrument``),
which the verification tutorial :doc:`/examples/instrument_tests` uses to check
every kernel, the convolution, the band integral, the front criteria and the
complete measurement against analytical results.
