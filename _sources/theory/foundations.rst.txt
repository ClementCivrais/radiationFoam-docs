Spectral quantities and conventions
===================================

This page fixes the quantities every other theory page uses: the
wavelength grid, the units, the emission and absorption coefficients,
the source function and the radiative transfer equation along a ray. It
ends with a map from each piece of physics to the library directory that
implements it, the place to start reading the code.

All of it lives in one library, ``libradiationFoam``
(``src/radiation``). The application ``radiationFoam``
(``applications/solvers/radiationFoam``) only builds the radiating
species, the population model and the continuum, selects a transport
mode and runs it.

Wavelength grid
---------------

Spectra are computed on a uniform grid of vacuum wavelengths,
``spectralGrid`` (``src/radiation/common/spectralGrid``):

.. math::

   \lambda_i = \lambda_{\min} + i\,\Delta\lambda,
   \qquad i = 0, \dots, N-1,

.. math::

   \Delta\lambda = \frac{\lambda_{\max} - \lambda_{\min}}{N - 1},

where :math:`\lambda_{\min}` and :math:`\lambda_{\max}` are the entries
``minWavelength`` and ``maxWavelength`` (given in nm, stored in m) and
:math:`N` is ``nPoints``. The constructor stops if
:math:`\lambda_{\max} \le \lambda_{\min}` or :math:`N < 100`.

.. code-block:: cpp

   spectralGrid
   {
       minWavelength   867.9;   // [nm]
       maxWavelength   869.2;   // [nm]
       nPoints         1000;    // [-], >= 100
   }

The singleCell mode reads one such grid; shockTube1D reads one per
spectral window (sub-dictionaries of ``windows``), see
:doc:`/theory/transport`.

Each grid point stands for a bin. The bin of point :math:`i` is
:math:`(\lambda_{i-1}, \lambda_i]` (with
:math:`\lambda_{-1} = \lambda_{\min} - \Delta\lambda`), and the value a
line gives to point :math:`i` is the integral of its profile over that bin
divided by :math:`\Delta\lambda`. A line inside the window therefore
integrates to exactly one on any grid, however narrow it is
(``src/radiation/radiators/basic/radiatingSpecies/radiatingSpecies.H``;
details in :doc:`/theory/line_shapes`).

Wavelengths and wavenumbers
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

All wavelengths are **vacuum** wavelengths; no air conversion is made
anywhere. Atomic line positions are read in Ångström from the database
and stored in metres (:math:`\times 10^{-10}`). Molecular line positions
are computed as wavenumbers :math:`\tilde\nu` in cm\ :sup:`-1` from term
values and placed at

.. math::

   \lambda = \frac{1}{100\,\tilde\nu} \quad [\mathrm{m}],
   \qquad \tilde\nu\ \text{in cm}^{-1}.

Energies in the data files are in cm\ :sup:`-1` and are converted to
joules by :math:`E = 100\,h c\,\tilde E`.

Physical constants
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The radiators and population models carry their own constants, the exact
SI 2019 values (OpenFOAM's ``DimensionedConstants`` hold six digits
only): :math:`k_B = 1.380649\times10^{-23}` J K\ :sup:`-1`,
:math:`h = 6.62607015\times10^{-34}` J s, :math:`c = 299792458` m s\ :sup:`-1`,
:math:`e = 1.602176634\times10^{-19}` C, and in ``diatomicBands`` the
CODATA 2018 atomic mass unit :math:`1.66053906660\times10^{-27}` kg.

Emission and absorption coefficients
------------------------------------

Every radiator adds two spectral fields on the grid
(``radiatingSpecies::addEmissionAbsorption``):

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 22 20 58

   * - Quantity
     - Units
     - Meaning
   * - :math:`j_\lambda`
     - W m\ :sup:`-3` sr\ :sup:`-1` m\ :sup:`-1`
     - spontaneous emission coefficient: power emitted per unit volume,
       solid angle and wavelength
   * - :math:`\kappa_\lambda`
     - m\ :sup:`-1`
     - absorption coefficient, **net of stimulated emission**
   * - :math:`I_\lambda`
     - W m\ :sup:`-2` sr\ :sup:`-1` m\ :sup:`-1`
     - spectral radiance (specific intensity)
   * - :math:`\int I_\lambda\,d\lambda`
     - W m\ :sup:`-2` sr\ :sup:`-1`
     - band-integrated radiance (``bands.csv`` of shockTube1D)
   * - :math:`4\pi\int j_\lambda\,d\lambda`
     - W m\ :sup:`-3`
     - emitted power per unit volume (``profile.csv`` of shockTube1D)

Both coefficients are **per unit wavelength**. The :math:`4\pi` appears
only in volumetric totals. The coefficients of the species and of the
continuum processes are summed:

.. math::

   j_\lambda = \sum_s j_{\lambda,s} + j_\lambda^{\mathrm{cont}},
   \qquad
   \kappa_\lambda = \sum_s \kappa_{\lambda,s} + \kappa_\lambda^{\mathrm{cont}}.

A bound-bound line :math:`u \to l` contributes a line-integrated
coefficient times a normalised profile :math:`\phi_\lambda`
(:math:`\int\phi_\lambda\,d\lambda = 1`, units m\ :sup:`-1`):

.. math::

   j_\lambda = \epsilon_{ul}\,\phi_\lambda(\lambda - \lambda_0),
   \qquad
   \kappa_\lambda = k_{ul}\,\phi_\lambda(\lambda - \lambda_0),

with

.. math::

   \epsilon_{ul} = \frac{h c}{4\pi\lambda_0}\,n_u A_{ul}
   \quad [\mathrm{W\,m^{-3}\,sr^{-1}}],

.. math::

   k_{ul} = \frac{\lambda_0^4}{8\pi c}\,A_{ul}\,g_u
            \left(\frac{n_l}{g_l} - \frac{n_u}{g_u}\right)
   \quad [\mathrm{m^{-1}\,m}].

Here :math:`n_u, n_l` are the level number densities (m\ :sup:`-3`),
:math:`g_u, g_l` their statistical weights and :math:`A_{ul}` the
Einstein coefficient for spontaneous emission (s\ :sup:`-1`). The second
term of :math:`k_{ul}` is stimulated emission. When the pair of levels is
inverted (:math:`n_u/g_u > n_l/g_l`), :math:`k_{ul} < 0`: the line is a
gain, and the code keeps the negative value. The atomic forms are in
:doc:`/theory/atomic_lines`, the molecular ones (written with band
strengths and Hoenl-London factors) in :doc:`/theory/diatomic_bands`.

Source function
---------------

The source function is the ratio

.. math::

   S_\lambda = \frac{j_\lambda}{\kappa_\lambda}.

For a single line, the two expressions above give

.. math::

   S_\lambda
     = \frac{2 h c^2}{\lambda^5}\,
       \frac{1}{\dfrac{n_l\,g_u}{n_u\,g_l} - 1},

which depends on the populations only through the ratio of the two
levels. When the two levels are in Boltzmann equilibrium at a temperature
:math:`T`, :math:`n_l g_u/(n_u g_l) = \exp(hc/\lambda k_B T)` and
:math:`S_\lambda` becomes the Planck function

.. math::

   B_\lambda(T) = \frac{2 h c^2}{\lambda^5}\,
                  \frac{1}{\exp\!\left(\dfrac{hc}{\lambda k_B T}\right) - 1},

i.e. Kirchhoff's law :math:`j_\lambda = \kappa_\lambda B_\lambda`. Out of
equilibrium the code never assumes this relation: :math:`j_\lambda` and
:math:`\kappa_\lambda` are computed separately from the upper and lower
populations of each line, whatever their origin
(:doc:`/theory/populations`). The source function is then the generalised
Kirchhoff form above, line by line.

Radiative transfer equation
---------------------------

Along a ray of path length :math:`s` the radiance obeys

.. math::

   \frac{dI_\lambda}{ds} = j_\lambda(s) - \kappa_\lambda(s)\,I_\lambda(s).

Scattering is not modelled. With the optical depth
:math:`\tau_\lambda(s) = \int_0^s \kappa_\lambda\,ds'`, the formal
solution for a ray entering with :math:`I_\lambda(0)` is

.. math::

   I_\lambda(s) = I_\lambda(0)\,e^{-\tau_\lambda(s)}
     + \int_0^s j_\lambda(s')\,
       e^{-[\tau_\lambda(s) - \tau_\lambda(s')]}\,ds'.

For a uniform slab of thickness :math:`D` and no incoming radiance, this
reduces to

.. math::

   I_\lambda = S_\lambda\left(1 - e^{-\kappa_\lambda D}\right)
             = -\frac{j_\lambda}{\kappa_\lambda}\,
               \mathrm{expm1}(-\kappa_\lambda D),

with the optically thin limit :math:`I_\lambda = j_\lambda D` when
:math:`\kappa_\lambda D \ll 1`. This is the form the shockTube1D mode
evaluates for each cell of a shock-tube profile (it uses ``expm1`` so the
thin limit is exact to round-off, and it holds for either sign of
:math:`\kappa_\lambda`); see :doc:`/theory/transport`. The instrument
model that turns this radiance into what a spectrometer records is in
:doc:`/theory/instrument`.

Map: physics to library directory
---------------------------------

A student who wants to see how a piece of physics is coded can start from
this table. All paths are under ``src/radiation/``.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 30 32 38

   * - Physics
     - Directory (classes)
     - Theory / classes pages
   * - Wavelength grid, bins
     - ``common/spectralGrid`` (``spectralGrid``)
     - this page, :doc:`/classes/common`
   * - State of one cell (n, T, n\ :sub:`e`, T\ :sub:`e`, level
       distribution)
     - ``common/radiationCellState`` (``radiationCellState``)
     - :doc:`/theory/populations`, :doc:`/classes/common`
   * - Spectroscopic levels a radiator needs
     - ``common/radiationLevels`` (``radiationLevels``)
     - :doc:`/theory/populations`, :doc:`/classes/common`
   * - Level populations from temperatures or from the DSMC
       distribution
     - ``populations/`` (``radiationPopulationModel``,
       ``boltzmannTemperatures``, ``cellDistribution``)
     - :doc:`/theory/populations`, :doc:`/classes/populations`
   * - Atomic bound-bound lines
     - ``radiators/derived/atomicLines``
     - :doc:`/theory/atomic_lines`, :doc:`/classes/radiators`
   * - Diatomic band systems, line by line
     - ``radiators/derived/diatomicBands``
     - :doc:`/theory/diatomic_bands`, :doc:`/classes/radiators`
   * - Line broadening and the Voigt profile
     - ``lineShapes/`` (``voigtWhitingOliveroLongbothum``)
     - :doc:`/theory/line_shapes`, :doc:`/classes/line_shapes`
   * - Bound-free and free-free continuum
     - ``continuum/`` (``boundFreePhysical``, ``freeFreePhysical``)
     - :doc:`/theory/continuum`, :doc:`/classes/continuum`
   * - Radiative transfer (single cell, 1-D shock tube)
     - ``transport/`` (``singleCell``, ``shockTube1D``)
     - :doc:`/theory/transport`, :doc:`/classes/transport`
   * - Cell states of a 1-D profile
     - ``profiles/shockTubeProfile``
     - :doc:`/classes/profiles`
   * - Spectrometer (instrument line shape, spatial response)
     - ``instrument/``
     - :doc:`/theory/instrument`, :doc:`/classes/instrument`
   * - Spectroscopic data and their sources
     - ``database/`` (data files, generators in ``database/sources``)
     - :doc:`/theory/database`, :doc:`/classes/database`

The data flow of one run is: the species are built once (levels and line
lists, ``radiators/``); for every cell, the population model gives the
number density of every level (``populations/``); each radiator adds its
:math:`j_\lambda`, :math:`\kappa_\lambda` with the line profiles
(``lineShapes/``), the continuum adds its own (``continuum/``); the
transport mode turns the coefficients into radiance (``transport/``) and,
optionally, into a simulated measurement (``instrument/``).
