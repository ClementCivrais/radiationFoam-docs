Diatomic band systems, line by line
===================================

A diatomic molecule radiates in band systems. An electronic transition
:math:`n' \to n''` (a *system*, e.g. the N\ :sub:`2` first positive
system B\ :sup:`3`\ :math:`\Pi_g` :math:`\to` A\ :sup:`3`\ :math:`\Sigma_u^+`)
splits into vibrational bands :math:`(v', v'')`, and each band into
rotational lines :math:`J' \to J''` grouped in branches. The radiator
``diatomicBands`` (``src/radiation/radiators/derived/diatomicBands``)
computes every rotational line of every band of a molecule file
explicitly: its position, its strength and the levels it connects. The
line list is built once at construction (``diatomicBandsRead.C`` reads the
file, ``diatomicBandsLines.C`` builds lines and levels); per cell only the
populations, widths and line placement are evaluated (``diatomicBands.C``).
Class and keywords: :doc:`/classes/radiators`.

The rovibronic levels are :math:`(n, v, J)`, the same identification as the
DSMC ladders of dsmcFoam, so the populations of the levels can come either
from temperatures or from the sampled distribution
(:doc:`/theory/populations`).

The **transition type** (1 to 6) selects the rotational structure: the
term-value formulas and the Hoenl-London factors. It is chosen from the
term symbols of the two states (section "Rotational structure" below).

Data of a molecule file
-----------------------

Per electronic state, 17 constants: :math:`g_e`, :math:`T_e`, :math:`D_0`,
:math:`\omega_e`, :math:`\omega_e x_e`, :math:`\omega_e y_e`,
:math:`\omega_e z_e`, :math:`B_e`, :math:`\alpha_e`, :math:`\mu`,
``nuspin``, ``altnat``, :math:`D_e`, :math:`\beta_e`, :math:`r_e`,
:math:`A` (spin-orbit constant) and :math:`\Lambda`. Energies are in
cm\ :sup:`-1` in the file and in J in the code; :math:`g_e =
(2S+1)(2-\delta_{0,\Lambda})`. Per band: upper and lower state, type and
an integer multiplier ``smf`` (1 in every shipped file). Per vibrational
band :math:`(v', v'')`, one row of the transition table: the squared
electronic transition moment :math:`R_e^2` [(e a\ :sub:`0`)\ :sup:`2`]
and the Franck-Condon factor :math:`q_{v'v''}`. The format is in
:doc:`/classes/database`.

Term values and line positions
------------------------------

Per vibrational band the code evaluates, with all constants in J and
:math:`x = v + \tfrac12`,

.. math::

   G(v) = \omega_e x - \omega_e x_e x^2 + \omega_e y_e x^3 + \omega_e z_e x^4,

.. math::

   B_v = B_e - \alpha_e x, \qquad D_v = D_e + \beta_e x,

and the band origin

.. math::

   \tilde\nu_0 = \frac{(T_e' - T_e'') + (G' - G'')}{100\,h c}
   \quad [\mathrm{cm^{-1}}].

:math:`G(v)` includes the zero-point energy and :math:`T_e` is measured
from the minimum of the ground state. A rotational line between the term
values :math:`F'(J')` and :math:`F''(J'')` of the two states (given per
type below) sits at

.. math::

   \tilde\nu = \tilde\nu_0 + \frac{F' - F''}{100\,h c},
   \qquad
   \lambda = \frac{1}{100\,\tilde\nu}\ [\mathrm{m}].

The rotational quantum number runs to

.. math::

   k_{\max} = \mathrm{INT}\!\left(\sqrt{\max\!\left(0,
       \tfrac14 + \frac{D_0'' - G''}{B_v''}\right) + 1}\right),

built from the **lower** state, with the :math:`+1` inside the root (the
NEQAIR85 form kept from the verified version). A band is skipped in a
window if :math:`2\lambda_0 < \lambda_{\min}` or
:math:`\lambda_0/2 > \lambda_{\max}`, :math:`\lambda_0 = 1/(100\tilde\nu_0)`.

These positions come from the four-term expansion of the molecule file,
not from the rovibronic energies :math:`E(n,v,J)` of the species database
of dsmcFoam. The expansion cannot hold the higher coefficients of the
source fits: the database README lists band-origin differences against
the IPAC tables of a few cm\ :sup:`-1` for low :math:`v` and up to about
:math:`10^3` cm\ :sup:`-1` for high :math:`v` of O\ :sub:`2`
Schumann-Runge (``src/radiation/database/README.md``, "Checks").

Line strength
-------------

Emission
~~~~~~~~

The line-integrated emission of a line of a band :math:`(v', v'')`, upper
level :math:`u = (n', v', J')`, is in cgs form

.. math::

   \epsilon_{ul} = \frac{16\pi^3 c\,\tilde\nu^4}{3}\;
     \frac{N_u}{g_u}\;
     \mathrm{AF}\;S_{J'J''}\;q_{v'v''}\,R_e^2\,\mathrm{smf}\,(e a_0)^2,

with :math:`N_u/g_u` the upper population per state, :math:`S_{J'J''}`
the Hoenl-London factor, AF the nuclear-spin alternation factor and
:math:`e a_0 = 2.5415785\times10^{-18}` esu cm. It follows from the
emission :math:`N_u A\,hc\tilde\nu/(4\pi)` with the dipole transition
probability of one line,
:math:`A = 64\pi^4\tilde\nu^3 S_{J'J''} q R_e^2 (ea_0)^2/(3h\,g_u)`. The
code writes it as

.. math::

   \epsilon_{ul} = 10^{16}\,\mathrm{AF}\,S_{J'J''}\,
     (\tilde\nu^2 e a_0)^2\,c_S\,\frac{n_u}{g_u},
   \qquad
   c_S = \frac{16\times10^{-5}\,c\,10^{-6}\,q_{v'v''}\,R_e^2\,
               \mathrm{smf}\,\pi^3}{3},

with :math:`n_u/g_u` in m\ :sup:`-3`, and places
:math:`\epsilon_{ul}/10^{10}\,\phi_\lambda` on the grid; the factors
:math:`16\times10^{-5}` (:math:`c` in cm s\ :sup:`-1` and erg to J),
:math:`10^{-6}` (m\ :sup:`-3` to cm\ :sup:`-3`), :math:`10^{16}` and
:math:`10^{10}` combine to W m\ :sup:`-3` sr\ :sup:`-1`
(m\ :sup:`-1` after the profile). For type 2, :math:`c_S` is halved
(below).

The convention for :math:`R_e^2` is the degeneracy-summed moment of Laux
and Kruger (PARADE "sumre2/fcf"): the database derives it from the band
Einstein coefficient as

.. math::

   R_e^2 = \frac{g_e'\,A_{v'v''}}{2.026\times10^{-6}\,
                 \tilde\nu_{v'v''}^3\,q_{v'v''}},

(Gilmore et al., 1992, Eq. 9), so that the lines of one upper level,
summed over branches, give the band emission
:math:`N_{v'} A_{v'v''}\,hc\tilde\nu/(4\pi)`.

Absorption
~~~~~~~~~~

The absorption follows from the emission by the Einstein relations
(generalised Kirchhoff law, :doc:`/theory/foundations`):

.. math::

   k_{ul} = \frac{\lambda^5}{2 h c^2}\;\frac{\epsilon_{ul}}{n_u/g_u}\;
            \left(\frac{n_l}{g_l} - \frac{n_u}{g_u}\right),

coded as :math:`\tilde\nu^{-5}\,10^{16}\,\mathrm{AF}\,S\,(\tilde\nu^2 e
a_0)^2 c_S\,(n_l/g_l - n_u/g_u)/(2\times10^{10} h c^2)` and divided by
:math:`10^{10}` on the grid. :math:`k_{ul}` is net of stimulated emission:
an inverted line has :math:`k_{ul} < 0` (gain), which is kept. The lower
population per state :math:`n_l/g_l` is given by the population model
(``lineLowerPopulation``; :doc:`/theory/populations`).

Which lines are placed
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

At construction a line is kept only if its strength and :math:`\tilde\nu`
are positive. Per cell, with ``boltzmannTemperatures`` a line is placed
only if :math:`\epsilon_{ul} \ge 10^{-25}` in the code's units before the
division by :math:`10^{10}` (i.e. :math:`10^{-35}` W m\ :sup:`-3`
sr\ :sup:`-1`), its absorption included. With ``cellDistribution`` a line
below that emission threshold (an empty or unsampled upper level) is
still placed for its absorption alone when :math:`|k_{ul}|` passes the
same threshold. The species is skipped in a cell with
:math:`n_s < 10` m\ :sup:`-3` or :math:`T_{\mathrm{trans}} < 10` K.

Rotational structure
--------------------

Branches
~~~~~~~~

A branch is the set of lines with the same :math:`\Delta J = J' - J''`:
P (:math:`J'' = J' + 1`), Q (:math:`J'' = J'`), R (:math:`J'' = J' - 1`).
In multiplet bands each spin component :math:`F_i` gives its own main
branches (:math:`P_1, R_2, \dots`) and the satellite branches between
different components (:math:`^{O}P_{12}, {}^{S}R_{21}, \dots`).

Hoenl-London factors and their normalisation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The Hoenl-London factor :math:`S_{J'J''}` is the rotational part of the
line strength. In the code it is normalised so that the lines leaving one
upper level, all its components together, sum to

.. math::

   \sum_{J''} S_{J'J''} = (2J'+1)\,\frac{\text{states of the level}}{g_e\,(2J'+1)},

which is :math:`2J'+1` for a level whose components all exist at
:math:`J'`. With this normalisation, the sum of the line emissions of a
level is the band emission :math:`N_{v'}A_{v'v''}hc\tilde\nu/4\pi`.

Types 1 and 2: singlet forms (Herzberg 1950)
""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""

**Type 1, parallel** (:math:`\Delta\Lambda = 0`), emission from
:math:`J' \ge \Lambda`, :math:`L = \Lambda`:

.. math::

   S_R = \frac{(J'+L)(J'-L)}{J'}, \qquad
   S_Q = \frac{(2J'+1)L^2}{J'(J'+1)}, \qquad
   S_P = \frac{(J'+1+L)(J'+1-L)}{J'+1},

with no Q branch for :math:`\Lambda = 0` (then :math:`S_R = J'`,
:math:`S_P = J'+1`). They sum to :math:`2J'+1`.

**Type 2, perpendicular** (:math:`\Delta\Lambda = \pm1`), emission from
:math:`J' \ge \Lambda'`, :math:`L = \Lambda'` if :math:`\Lambda' <
\Lambda''`, else :math:`L = -\Lambda'`:

.. math::

   S_P = \frac{(J'+1+L)(J'+2+L)}{J'+1}, \qquad
   S_Q = \frac{(J'-L)(2J'+1)(J'+1+L)}{J'(J'+1)}, \qquad
   S_R = \frac{(J'-L)(J'-1-L)}{J'}.

These forms sum to :math:`2(2J'+1)` per upper level: they count both
:math:`\Lambda`-doubling components of the :math:`\Pi` (or
:math:`\Delta`) state. The code therefore halves :math:`c_S` for every
type-2 band, the factor :math:`(2-\delta_{0,\Lambda'+\Lambda''}) = 2`
(figure, panel a).

Types 3, 5, 6: matrix method in the Hund's case (a) basis
"""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""

For multiplet states (doublets and triplets with :math:`\Lambda > 0`) the
coupling between electron spin and rotation changes with :math:`J`: from
Hund's case (a) (spin coupled to the internuclear axis, large
:math:`Y = A/B_v`) at low :math:`J` towards case (b) (spin coupled to the
rotation) at high :math:`J`. The code covers all intermediate couplings
with the matrix method (Arnold, Whiting and Lyle 1969; Whiting and
Nicholls 1974; Kovács 1969, ch. 3). For a state at :math:`J` it builds the
rotational Hamiltonian of Hill and Van Vleck (1928) in the case (a) basis
:math:`|\Lambda, \Sigma, \Omega = \Lambda + \Sigma\rangle`,
:math:`\Sigma = -S, \dots, S`, restricted to :math:`|\Omega| \le J`,

.. math::

   \langle\Omega|H|\Omega\rangle
     = B_v\left[J(J+1) - \Omega^2 + S(S+1) - \Sigma^2\right]
       + A\Lambda\Sigma,

.. math::

   \langle\Omega|H|\Omega+1\rangle
     = -B_v\sqrt{J(J+1) - \Omega(\Omega+1)}\,
            \sqrt{S(S+1) - \Sigma(\Sigma+1)},

and diagonalises it (Jacobi rotations, basis size :math:`\le 3`,
:math:`S \le 1`). The eigenvalues are the term values of the spin
components; the eigenvectors :math:`c(\Omega)` give the mixing. The
strength of the line between eigenstate :math:`u` at :math:`J'` and
eigenstate :math:`l` at :math:`J''` is

.. math::

   S_{J'J''} = \frac{2J'+1}{(2S+1)(2-\delta_{0,\Lambda'+\Lambda''})}
     \sum_{\sigma}\left|\sum_{\Sigma}
       c_u(\Omega')\,c_l(\Omega'')\,
       \langle J', -\Omega';\, 1, q \,|\, J'', -\Omega''\rangle
     \right|^2,

with :math:`q = \Omega' - \Omega''` (:math:`\Sigma` conserved by the
dipole operator), :math:`\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle` a Clebsch-Gordan
coefficient (Condon-Shortley table) and :math:`\sigma` the two signs of a
:math:`\Lambda > 0` state (the :math:`\Lambda`-doubling components, merged
in the levels; for :math:`\Pi`-:math:`\Pi` only the same sign
contributes). Every line between the components that exist at
:math:`J'` and :math:`J'' = J', J' \pm 1` is computed, satellites
included, and dropped only if :math:`S < 10^{-12}(2J'+1)`. The limits of
this formula are Herzberg's singlet forms, the pure case (a) and case (b)
forms, and Earls (1935) for :math:`^2\Sigma`-:math:`^2\Pi`.

The **line positions** of these types use closed-form term values; each
eigenstate is matched to the closed-form root nearest in energy. With
:math:`Y = A/B_v`:

* type 3, :math:`^2\Sigma`-:math:`^2\Pi` (no centrifugal term):

  .. math::

     F_{1,2} = B_v\left[(J+\tfrac12)^2 - \Lambda^2
       \mp \tfrac12\sqrt{4(J+\tfrac12)^2 + Y(Y-4)\Lambda^2}\right];

* type 5, :math:`^2\Pi`-:math:`^2\Pi`: the same with
  :math:`-D_v J^4` (:math:`F_1`) and :math:`-D_v (J+1)^4` (:math:`F_2`);
* type 6, :math:`^3\Pi`-:math:`^3\Pi` (Budó):

  .. math::

     F_1 = B_v\left[J(J+1) - \sqrt{Z_1} - 2Z_2\right] - D_v (J-\tfrac12)^4,

  .. math::

     F_2 = B_v\left[J(J+1) + 4Z_2\right] - D_v (J+\tfrac12)^4,

  .. math::

     F_3 = B_v\left[J(J+1) + \sqrt{Z_1} - 2Z_2\right] - D_v (J+\tfrac32)^4,

  .. math::

     Z_1 = \Lambda^2 Y(Y-4) + \tfrac43 + 4J(J+1),
     \qquad
     Z_2 = \frac{\Lambda^2 Y(Y-1) - \tfrac49 - 2J(J+1)}{3 Z_1}.

The eigenvalues of the matrix equal the type-3 and type-5 closed forms
exactly (without :math:`D_v`), and Budó's type-6 forms within
0.6 cm\ :sup:`-1` (code comment, ``diatomicBandsLines.C``). Panel (b) of
the figure shows the two components of NO X :sup:`2`\ :math:`\Pi`
(:math:`Y = 72.7`, close to case (a)): at :math:`J = 1/2` only the
:math:`\Omega = 1/2` component exists.

Type 4: :math:`^2\Sigma`-:math:`^2\Sigma`
"""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""

Both states are case (b). The levels are keyed by the integer
:math:`J = N`, and six branches are computed: :math:`R_1, R_2, P_1, P_2`
and the satellites :math:`F_2' \to F_1''` and :math:`F_1' \to F_2''`. The
strengths are those of the matrix method above, evaluated at the true
:math:`J` of each component (:math:`F_1`: :math:`J = N + \tfrac12`,
:math:`F_2`: :math:`J = N - \tfrac12`). The positions use the type-5 term
form with the integer :math:`J`:

.. math::

   F_\mp = B_v\left[(J+\tfrac12)^2 - \Lambda^2
       \mp \tfrac12\sqrt{4(J+\tfrac12)^2 + Y(Y-4)\Lambda^2}\right]
       - D_v (J + o)^4,
   \qquad o \in \{0, 1\}.

Type 1 term values and triplet splitting
""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""

For type 1 the positions are

.. math::

   F = B_v\left[J(J+1) + 4 Z_2\right] - D_v (J+\tfrac12)^4,

with :math:`Z_2` (as for type 6) only when the **upper** state is a
triplet (:math:`g_e = 3` for :math:`\Lambda = 0`, :math:`g_e/2 = 3`
otherwise), applied to both states; :math:`Z_2 = 0` otherwise. This is the
O\ :sub:`2` Schumann-Runge case (B :sup:`3`\ :math:`\Sigma_u^-`).

Type 2 term values
""""""""""""""""""

For type 2, :math:`F = B_v J(J+1) - D_v (J+\tfrac12)^4`, with no spin
splitting, even for triplets: the N\ :sub:`2` first positive system
(B :sup:`3`\ :math:`\Pi_g \to` A :sup:`3`\ :math:`\Sigma_u^+`) is computed
as a singlet-like perpendicular band, and the NO :math:`\beta'` system
(B' :sup:`2`\ :math:`\Delta \to` X :sup:`2`\ :math:`\Pi`) with
:math:`\Lambda' = 2` at the spin-averaged positions.

.. figure:: /_static/figures/diatomic_bands/hoenl_london_terms.png
   :width: 100%

   (a) Type-2 Hoenl-London factors of a :sup:`1`\ :math:`\Pi \to`
   :sup:`1`\ :math:`\Sigma` band and their sum :math:`2(2J'+1)` per upper

Parity, nuclear spin and the alternation factor
-----------------------------------------------

In a homonuclear molecule (N\ :sub:`2`, N\ :sub:`2`\ :sup:`+`,
O\ :sub:`2`) the exchange symmetry of the nuclei gives the rotational
levels of a :math:`\Sigma` state alternating statistical weights. The
code applies a per-line alternation factor

.. math::

   \mathrm{AF} = 1 + \frac{(-1)^{\mathrm{NINT}(J'' + a')}}{2I'' + 1},

with :math:`a'` the entry ``altnat`` of the **upper** state and
:math:`I''` the entry ``nuspin`` (nuclear spin) of the **lower** state;
AF = 1 if ``altnat`` = 0. For :sup:`14`\ N (:math:`I = 1`) AF is
:math:`4/3` or :math:`2/3`, mean 1; for :sup:`16`\ O (:math:`I = 0`) it is
2 or 0, i.e. every second rotational level of the lower state is missing.
Because the mean of AF is one, the partition function has no symmetry
number (:doc:`/theory/populations`).

The database chooses ``altnat`` so that the lines favour the lower-state
rotational levels that are symmetric under exchange of the (boson)
nuclei: 2 (even :math:`N''`) for :math:`\Sigma_g^+` and
:math:`\Sigma_u^-` lower states, 1 (odd :math:`N''`) for
:math:`\Sigma_u^+` and :math:`\Sigma_g^-`, 0 for a :math:`\Pi` lower
state (both parities at each :math:`J`), for heteronuclear NO and for
states that are never an upper state. The alternation of a type-3 band is
not defined: a type-3 band with ``altnat'`` :math:`\neq 0` is a fatal
error, and the N\ :sub:`2`\ :sup:`+` Meinel upper state has ``altnat`` 0
(no line-by-line alternation, band-summed strength unchanged).

:math:`\Lambda`-doubling (the two parity components :math:`e/f` of a
:math:`\Lambda > 0` state) is not resolved: the two components are one
level with weight :math:`(2-\delta_{0,\Lambda})`, and their lines are
summed in the strengths.

Levels and statistical weights
------------------------------

Each line creates (or reuses) its upper and lower level, keyed by
(state, :math:`v`, :math:`J`), the key the population models match:

* types 1, 2, 4 (:math:`J` the rotational quantum number of a
  singlet-like or case (b) treatment): :math:`g = g_e(2J+1)`;
* types 3, 5, 6 (spin components resolved with their own :math:`J`):
  :math:`g = (2-\delta_{0,\Lambda})\,n(J)\,(2J+1)`, with :math:`n(J)` the
  number of spin components with :math:`|\Omega| \le J`, e.g. 4 instead
  of 8 at :math:`J = 1/2` of a :math:`^2\Pi` state.

The first band that creates a level sets its weight. A half-integer
:math:`J` is keyed by :math:`J - \tfrac12` (the rotational index of the
DSMC ladder), except when (state, :math:`v`, :math:`J-\tfrac12`) is also
the key of an integer-:math:`J` level of different energy, as for
N\ :sub:`2`\ :sup:`+` X :sup:`2`\ :math:`\Sigma_g^+`, lower state of both
the first negative band (type 4, integer :math:`J = N`) and the Meinel
band (type 3, half-integer :math:`J`): the half-integer level is then
keyed "state v 2J/2" (e.g. ``X2Sigma 0 5/2``). The energy parts of each
level are :math:`E_{\mathrm{el}} = T_e`, :math:`E_{\mathrm{vib}} = G(v)`,
:math:`E_{\mathrm{rot}} = B_v J(J+1)`, the energies of the populations
from temperatures. A level that two bands reach with different energies
is a fatal error.

Line widths
-----------

The molecular widths (FWHM, m) are evaluated once per vibrational band at
its origin :math:`\lambda_0 = 0.01/\tilde\nu_0`, with :math:`M = m/m_u`,
:math:`n` the radiator density and :math:`T` its translational
temperature:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 18 82

   * - Width
     - Expression
   * - Doppler
     - :math:`7.16\times10^{-7}\,\lambda_0\sqrt{T/M}`
   * - self
     - :math:`1.33\times10^{-29}\sqrt{2/M}\,\lambda_0^2\sqrt{T}\,n\,
       \times10^{-6}\times10\times10^{10}`
   * - foreign gas
     - :math:`5.85\times10^{-30}\sqrt{1/M + 1/M_f}\,\lambda_0^2\sqrt{T}\,n_f
       \times10^{-6}\times10\times10^{10}`
   * - natural
     - :math:`1.18\times10^{-14}` (constant)
   * - Stark
     - :math:`10^{-8}\times0.3\,(10^{-22}\max(n_e, 10^{-6}))^{0.6}\,
       \lambda_0^2\times10^{10}`

with :math:`n_f = \max(p/k_BT - n, 10^{-40}p/k_BT)`,
:math:`p = \sum_j n_j k_B T_j + n_e k_B T_e`, and the Voigt width
:math:`w_L/2 + \sqrt{w_L^2/4 + w_G^2}`. These are the PARADE cgs/Ångström
forms of the verified version; the foreign-gas molar mass is compared
with bounds in mismatched units and ends at 10 (pure radiator) or 28.85
(any mixture), the Stark width has no :math:`T_e` dependence. The
profile and its placement are in :doc:`/theory/line_shapes`.

Forms kept from the verified version
------------------------------------

The class header (``diatomicBands.H``) lists the forms of the verified
version that are kept on purpose, because correcting them changes the
spectra and is a separate decision. The main ones:

1. The transition-table reader skips exactly one record after the row
   count; with more comment records before the data, the first rows are
   undefined (taken as zero) and the last rows unread. The shipped files
   have one header record per table, so every row is read.
2. :math:`k_{\max}` from the lower state, :math:`+1` inside the root.
3. Populations use :math:`B_v J(J+1)` only; positions and the lower
   population of ``boltzmannTemperatures`` use the full :math:`F`.
4. Type 1: triplet terms of both states switched on by the upper state;
   :math:`Z_2` with :math:`-4/9` (sign not confirmed).
5. Type 2 has no spin splitting.
6. Type 4 uses integer :math:`J` (:math:`= N`) in the half-integer term
   forms.
7. Types 4-6 refuse :math:`k_{\max} > 500`.
8. :math:`G(v)` with :math:`+\omega_e z_e`.
9. Molecular widths as above (PARADE factors, foreign-gas mass, constant
   natural width, Stark without :math:`T_e`).
10. ``smf`` rounded to an integer.
