The spectroscopic database

Every number a radiator uses (level energies and weights, line wavelengths and Einstein coefficients, Stark widths, molecular constants, band strengths, photoionisation cross sections) comes from the radiation database, src/radiation/database. This page explains what it contains, where each value comes from and how it is regenerated. The file formats and the generator scripts are in Database files and generators.

Two principles

Provenance: every datum is referenced

Every level, line or band of the database references the article or database it came from, so it can be retrieved again (src/radiation/database/README.md, “Provenance rule”):

  • each data file starts with a // comment header that names the source keys it uses and, for database downloads, the exact query URL, the database version, the retrieval date and the SHA256 of the stored response; for articles and theses, table and page;

  • SOURCES.md has one entry per source key: full citation, DOI or URL, terms of use;

  • the raw downloaded responses are kept unchanged in sources/raw/ (they are the record; a new download goes into a new file and MANIFEST.json);

  • the data files are generated by the scripts in sources/ and never edited by hand;

  • a value with no source is allowed only as a declared placeholder, listed under “Gaps” in the README.

One database shared with dsmcFoam

The radiance computed from DSMC populations is meaningful only if the two codes describe the same levels. The database is therefore built to be shared with dsmcFoam (DSMC_Rovibronic repository) rather than duplicated:

  • Atoms. The dsmcFoam radiation coupling (electron-impact excitation, radiative decay and the writer of the cell distributions) uses level maps and multiplet tables generated from atoms/*.dat of this database (generators in the DSMC_Rovibronic repository, src/lagrangian/DSMC/radiationCoupling/database/sources/, reading this checkout through $RADIATIONFOAM_DIR; their headers record the radiationFoam commit they were built from). A DSMC atomic level is thus written in the distribution files with the labels of the radiation levels it covers, and cellDistribution maps it back without any conversion (Level populations). They must be regenerated after any change of atoms/.

  • Diatomics. Levels are keyed by \((n, v, J)\) on both sides; the writer of the distributions uses the electronic-state labels of the molecule files. NO.dat takes the dissociation energies, the equilibrium distances and the zero of the X 2\(\Pi\) state of its Laux (1993) states from the version-2 species database of dsmcFoam (build_molecular.py reads it through $DSMC_ROVIBRONIC_DIR).

  • What is not yet shared. The diatomic line positions and the populations from temperatures use the four-term Dunham expansion of the molecule files, not the rovibronic energies \(E(n,v,J)\) of the species database; the README lists this as a gap (“port: species-database E(n,v,J)”). Some states are also absent from one side: N2 b 1\(\Pi_u\) is in no DSMC ladder, and b’, c3, c’4, o3 are in the version-2 ladder only, so the levels of these states have no DSMC population in the cell-distribution route (README, “N2 VUV systems”).

    Closing this gap is step 6 of the unified-database plan (docs/Development/UNIFIED_DATABASE_SPEC.md of the DSMC_Rovibronic repository, section C.3): radiating-species models that read the version-2 species database, with line positions \(E_u + \delta_u - E_l - \delta_l\) (shared energies plus the fine-structure offsets) and start-up checks of the distribution keys and partition functions. Phase 1 of that plan (the version-2 generator and libdsmcSpectroscopy) is built but not merged; the radiationFoam side follows it. The size of the gap today is measured in verification/dbConsistency (bands_summary.csv, molecules_levels_summary.csv): mapped levels agree at \(v = 0\) to 0.4 cm-1, but high-\(J\) energies differ by up to 8700 cm-1 (N2+ X), and in the EAST 50-29 UV/Vis window 18 % of the emission sits on lines displaced by more than the camera resolution.

Levels are J-resolved and sorted by energy; all ASD levels with a numeric energy and weight are kept, including the autoionising levels above the first ionisation limit (the level cut of the population models removes them, Level populations). Wavelengths are the ASD Ritz vacuum wavelengths at all wavelengths (query show_av=3; the generator refuses a response with an air-wavelength header). Only electric-dipole lines with a transition probability are included; forbidden lines (e.g. O I 557.7 and 630.0 nm) are not.

Photoionisation cross sections

atoms/N_I_photoionisation.dat and atoms/O_I_photoionisation.dat give, for every level of N_I.dat and O_I.dat, the photoionisation cross section read by the continuum class boundFreePhysical (Continuum radiation). Since 2026-11-16 they hold the total photoionisation cross sections of the R-matrix calculations of Nahar and Pradhan (1997, N I) and Nahar (1998, O I), as distributed by NORAD-Atomic-Data, averaged over photon-energy bins (0.02 eV up to 2 eV above threshold, 0.1 eV up to 12 eV, then logarithmic): each J-resolved ASD level takes the LS state of the same parent term, outer orbital and term, with its table moved so that every channel opens at the ASD binding energy (N 226 of 366 levels, O 154 of 580; the others, jK-coupled 4f labels, n > 10 and other parents, are hydrogenic). The former default, Johnston’s (2006) fits of the TOPbase cross sections (step models for his levels 1-3 and \(\sigma = \sigma_{\mathrm{th}}(h\nu_{\mathrm{th}}/h\nu)^\theta\) for the others), is kept as atoms/*_photoionisation_Johnston2006.dat; its O table (B-4) is misaligned as printed (row k+3 holds level k), which made the O excited-level cross sections wrong by orders of magnitude (O 3p zero in the visible, O 3s and 4s 10-130 times too large).

The systems, their transition types and their rotational structure are in Diatomic band systems, line by line. Each molecule file holds only the states of its bands plus the ground state, so a partition function summed over the file omits the other electronic states, a small effect below about 10 000 K (N2 B’, a’, w, e’ together: 0.13 % of the N2 VUV emission at 10 000 K, 2.7 % at 15 000 K, README).

Conventions of the molecular data

  • \(T_e\) is measured from the minimum of the species’ ground state (the IPAC tables measure it from \(v = 0\) of the neutral ground state, so it is shifted; e.g. N2 B 3\(\Pi_g\): 58443.2 + 1175.7 = 59618.9 cm-1).

  • \(G(v)\) and \(B(v)\) keep the first four and two coefficients (the PARADE expansion); the dropped higher coefficients are listed per state in each file.

  • \(R_e^2\) is the degeneracy-summed squared transition moment, derived from the IPAC Einstein coefficient by direct integration, \(R_e^2 = g_e' A_{v'v''}/(2.026\times10^{-6}\tilde\nu^3 q)\) (Gilmore et al. 1992, Eq. 9).

  • Only bands with \(\tilde\nu > 0\), \(q > 0\), \(A > 0\) are written; reverse bands (lower level above the upper one) are dropped.

  • \(g_e\), \(\Lambda\), nuspin (IAEA: 14N 1, 16O 0) and altnat are derived from the term symbols and the nuclear spins.

Regenerating the database

The data files are outputs of the generators. To rebuild (no network needed; fetch only downloads missing raw files):

cd $RADIATIONFOAM_DIR/src/radiation/database/sources
python3 build_atomic_nist.py build          # ../atoms/*.dat
python3 build_photoionisation.py build      # ../atoms/*_photoionisation.dat
DSMC_ROVIBRONIC_DIR=<DSMC_Rovibronic checkout> \
    python3 build_molecular.py build        # ../molecules/*.dat

build_molecular.py needs DSMC_ROVIBRONIC_DIR for NO.dat only (the version-2 species database). After a change of atoms/, the level maps and multiplet tables of the dsmcFoam coupling must be regenerated in the DSMC_Rovibronic repository. Details of each script: Database files and generators.

Two raw sources stay off git: the Gilmore et al. (1992) PDF (a copyrighted article) and the IPAC tables (no licence stated). Their URLs, retrieval times and SHA256 are in sources/raw/molecules/MANIFEST.json, so build_molecular.py fetch retrieves the same files and the hashes check them.

Known gaps

The README keeps the list of values without a source. The ones that matter most for a user:

  • Stark widths: none for N II and O II, and none for the ASD lines newer than Johnston’s tables (165 N I, 12 O I lines): \(w_S = 0\).

  • The bound-free Gaunt factor column of the atomic files is 0 (no source; not used by the continuum classes).

  • \(\beta_e\) and some \(D_e\) are 0 where Huber and Herzberg give none.

  • The PARADE \(R_e^2\) of the N2 Lyman-Birge-Hopfield band is 0.5 times the Gilmore value in this database’s convention (likely the same for the other \(\Pi\)-upper VUV systems); imported as they are.

  • Band origins of the four-term expansion differ from the IPAC origins at high \(v\) (up to about \(10^3\) cm-1 for O2 Schumann-Runge).
