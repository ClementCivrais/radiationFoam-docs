Atomic bound-bound lines

An atom or atomic ion radiates in lines: a transition from an upper level \(u\) to a lower level \(l\) emits a photon of wavelength \(\lambda_0 = hc/(E_u - E_l)\), and the reverse transition absorbs one. The radiator atomicLines (src/radiation/radiators/derived/atomicLines) computes the emission and absorption of every such line of a species from a list of levels and lines, with the level populations of the population model (Level populations) and a Voigt profile per line (Line shapes and broadening). Class and keywords: Radiating species.

Data per level and per line

The atomic file of the database (The spectroscopic database) gives, per level, its statistical weight \(g = 2J+1\), its energy \(E\) (cm-1) and an index; and, per line,

Quantity

Meaning

\(\lambda_0\)

line centre, vacuum wavelength (Ångström in the file; the NIST ASD Ritz wavelength)

\(l\), \(u\)

indices of the lower and upper levels

\(g_l^{\mathrm{line}}\), \(g_u^{\mathrm{line}}\)

statistical weights of the two levels as given with the line

\(A_{ul}\)

Einstein coefficient for spontaneous emission [s-1]

\(w_S\)

Stark half width at half maximum [Å] at \(T_e = 10^4\) K and \(n_e = 10^{16}\) cm-3

The line centre is taken from the file, not recomputed from the level energies. Lines are electric-dipole (E1) lines only.

Einstein coefficients and oscillator strengths

The three Einstein coefficients of a line are linked by detailed balance. \(A_{ul}\) is the probability per unit time that an atom in \(u\) decays spontaneously to \(l\); absorption and stimulated emission follow from it, which is why the file needs \(A_{ul}\) only. The absorption oscillator strength often quoted in tables is

\[f_{lu} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 m_e c\,\lambda_0^2}{2\pi e^2}\, \frac{g_u}{g_l}\,A_{ul},\]

the relation the code uses in its comments to convert widths written with \(f_{lu}\) (e.g. the resonance width below). The database stores \(A_{ul}\), not \(f_{lu}\).

Emission and absorption of one line

With \(n_u\), \(n_l\) the level populations [m-3] from the population model and \(g(u)\), \(g(l)\) the weights of the level list, the line-integrated coefficients are (atomicLines.C)

\[\epsilon_{ul} = \frac{h c}{4\pi}\, \frac{g_u^{\mathrm{line}}}{g(u)}\, \frac{n_u A_{ul}}{\lambda_0} \quad [\mathrm{W\,m^{-3}\,sr^{-1}}],\]
\[k_{ul} = \frac{\lambda_0^4 A_{ul}}{8\pi c} \left(\frac{g_u^{\mathrm{line}}}{g(l)}\,n_l - \frac{g_u^{\mathrm{line}}}{g(u)}\,n_u\right) \quad [\mathrm{m^{-1}\,m}],\]

and the spectral coefficients on the grid are

\[j_\lambda(\lambda_i) \mathrel{+}= \max(0, \epsilon_{ul})\,\phi_i, \qquad \kappa_\lambda(\lambda_i) \mathrel{+}= k_{ul}\,\phi_i,\]

with \(\phi_i\) [m-1] the bin-integrated, area-normalised line profile at grid point \(i\).

Why these forms. The emission is the number of decays per unit volume and time, \(n_u A_{ul}\), times the photon energy \(hc/\lambda_0\), shared equally over \(4\pi\) sr. The absorption is the absorption rate minus the stimulated-emission rate, each written with \(A_{ul}\) through the Einstein relations; with \(f_{lu}\) it reads

\[k_{ul} = \frac{e^2\lambda_0^2}{4\varepsilon_0 m_e c^2}\,f_{lu} \left(n_l - \frac{g_l}{g_u}\,n_u\right),\]

the classical form \(\pi r_e \lambda_0^2 f_{lu}\,n_l\) corrected for stimulated emission (\(r_e\) the classical electron radius).

Stimulated emission and line gain

The second term of \(k_{ul}\) is stimulated emission; \(k_{ul}\) is the net absorption. When the populations per state are inverted, \(n_u/g_u > n_l/g_l\), \(k_{ul} < 0\): the line amplifies instead of absorbing. The code keeps the negative value, as for the bound-free continuum, because the slab radiance \(-(j/\kappa)\,\mathrm{expm1}(-\kappa D)\) holds for either sign (Radiative transfer). Boltzmann populations never invert; inverted pairs can occur only with cellDistribution, from the DSMC sampling. The emission is clipped at zero (it is non-negative for any physical population).

Lumped levels and the ratio \(g^{\mathrm{line}}/g\)

The factors \(g_u^{\mathrm{line}}/g(u)\) and \(g_u^{\mathrm{line}}/g(l)\) allow a level list in which fine-structure levels are lumped into one level while the lines stay resolved: the line then sees the share \(g_u^{\mathrm{line}}/g(u)\) of the lumped level’s states. In the shipped files every level is J-resolved and the line weights equal the level weights (checked on all four atomic files), so the ratios are one and the forms reduce to those of Spectral quantities and conventions.

Multiplets

A multiplet is the group of lines between the fine-structure components \(J\) of two terms, e.g. the N I 3s 4P – 3p 4Do multiplet near 868 nm used by the singleCell_N tutorial. atomicLines computes every component as its own line; there is no multiplet lumping. Which lines are computed

For each cell the species is skipped if \(n_s < 10\) m-3 or \(T_{\mathrm{trans}} < 10\) K, or if the population model returns no population. Then, line by line:

  1. A line whose upper level lies above the bound limit, \(E_u > E_b\) (the lowered ionisation limit returned by the population model), is skipped: the upper level is not a bound state.

  2. A line farther than \(100\max(w_L, w_D) + \Delta\lambda\) from the grid is skipped (\(w_L\), \(w_D\) the Lorentz and Doppler widths below).

  3. Otherwise the profile is placed on the grid points of its footprint, \(\pm 25\) Voigt widths, each point receiving the profile integral over its own bin (Line shapes and broadening).

Note that the bound limit is \(E_b = E_{\mathrm{ion}} - \Delta E_{\mathrm{ion}}\) with \(E_{\mathrm{ion}}\) the entry ionisationEnergy. A species given ionisationEnergy 0 has no bound limit (\(E_b\) = GREAT): every level is kept and every line is computed, so the entry switches the level cut off. Give the ionisation energy of the species for physical results; until 2026-11-16 the value 0 made \(E_b \le 0\) and suppressed every line.

Line widths

The profile of each line is a Voigt profile built from a Gaussian (Doppler) and a Lorentzian width. atomicLines computes the widths (full widths at half maximum, m); the profile, its Voigt width and its placement are described in Line shapes and broadening. With \(\lambda_0\) in m, \(n\) the emitter density, \(T_{\mathrm{tr}}\) its translational temperature and \(m\) its mass:

Expression

Source (as cited in the code)

Stark

\(2\,n_e\,w_S\,(T_e/10^4)^{n_S}\times10^{-32}\)

\(20\,(\lambda_0^2/c)\,4.5214\times10^{-18}\,n_{\mathrm{tot}} (3k_B T_{\mathrm{tr}} n_{\mathrm{tot}}/\rho)^{0.3}\)

form of the verified version

natural

\(\dfrac{\lambda_0^2}{2\pi c}(\Gamma_u + \Gamma_l)\)

Laux (1993) Eq. 4.19

phase (impact)

\(\dfrac{\lambda_0^2}{\pi c}\,\dfrac{\nu_{\mathrm{coll}}}{n}\)

impact theory

resonance

\(\dfrac{3}{16\pi^3 c}\sqrt{g_u/g_l}\,\lambda_0^5 A_{ul}\, \dfrac{g_l}{g(l)}\,n(l)\)

Griem (1964) via Laux (1993) Eq. 4.20

Doppler

\(\sqrt{8k_B\ln 2\,T_{\mathrm{tr}}/m}\;\lambda_0/c\)

(ln 2 coded as 0.69315)

with \(w_L\) = Stark + van der Waals + natural + phase + resonance. In the Stark width, \(n_e\) [m-3] is the cell electron density, \(T_e\) the cell electron temperature, or the temperature of the population model when the cell gives none, and \(n_S\) the entry starkExponent (1/3 for most N and O lines, Park 1982 as quoted by Johnston 2006); \(n_e = 0\) gives no Stark broadening. The natural width uses the decay rate of each level,

\[\Gamma_x = \sum_{\text{lines leaving } x} \frac{g_u^{\mathrm{line}}}{g(x)}\,A,\]

the mean decay rate per state of a (possibly lumped) level. The phase width uses the hard-sphere collision rate of the emitter with every radiating species \(j\) of the cell,

\[\nu_{\mathrm{coll}} = \sum_j 2\,n\,n_j\,\pi(r + r_j)^2 \sqrt{\frac{2 k_B T_m (m + m_j)}{\pi m m_j}},\]

with \(r\) the entry radius and \(T_m\) the mass-density weighted mean translational temperature of the pair. Only the radiating species of the case are perturbers: a species of the mixture without a radiator does not broaden the lines.
