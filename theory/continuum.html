Continuum radiation

Besides the lines, a partially ionised gas emits and absorbs over wide wavelength ranges through two continuum processes:

  • bound-free: photoionisation of an atom in level \(i\), \(\mathrm{A}(i) + h\nu \to \mathrm{A}^+ + e^-\), and its reverse, radiative recombination of an electron onto level \(i\);

  • free-free: bremsstrahlung, the emission of a free electron deflected in the Coulomb field of an ion, and its reverse, inverse bremsstrahlung.

Both are off by default. Sources: src/radiation/continuum (continuumModel, continuumProcesses, boundFreePhysical, freeFreePhysical); the classes and their keywords are in Continuum classes.

Selection

The entry continuum of system/radiationProperties (top level, overridable per species) takes two values:

  • none (default): no continuum; the spectra are those of the bound-bound classes alone.

  • physical: for every atomic species (model atomicLines)

    • a boundFreePhysical process if its continuumCoeffs give an ionSpecies (the species one charge higher, which must itself be a radiating species of the case);

    • a freeFreePhysical process if its chargeNumber is 1 or more (the species is an ion).

Molecules have no continuum: a species entry continuum physical on a non-atomic species stops with an error; the global entry skips them. For N, O, N+ and O+, the neutrals carry the bound-free processes (with ionSpecies N+ and O+) and the ions carry the free-free processes.

The coefficients of all processes are added to the bound-bound \(j_\lambda\) and \(\kappa_\lambda\) at every grid point (continuumProcesses::addEmissionAbsorption), before the transfer (Radiative transfer). There is no profile and no placement: the continuum is evaluated directly at each grid wavelength.

Both processes use the free electrons of the cell state (\(n_e\), \(T_e\), Maxwellian at \(T_e\)). A cell with \(T_e \le 0\) has no continuum, whatever \(n_e\).

Bound-free continuum

Photoionisation cross sections

The process is level-resolved: every bound level \(i\) of the atom (the levels of its transition file) has its own cross section \(\sigma_i(\nu)\) [m2] and its own population \(n_i\) from the population model (Level populations), the same populations as for the lines. A photon ionises level \(i\) only above the threshold

\[h\nu \ge E_b - E_i ,\]

where \(E_b\) is the bound limit of the population model (the lowered ionisation limit, the same as for the lines; if the model gives \(E_b \le 0\) or \(E_b\) above the ionisation energy, the ionisation energy is used). Levels with \(E_i \ge E_b\) are not bound and are skipped.

Two cross-section models are available (entry crossSection):

hydrogenic (Kramers). The cross section of a hydrogen-like level of effective principal quantum number \(n_\mathrm{eff}\) (Zel’dovich and Raizer 1966, as written by Johnston 2006, Eqs. 3.34-3.35, p. 70):

\[\sigma_i(\nu) = C_K\,\frac{Z^{4}\,G}{\nu^{3}\,n_\mathrm{eff}^{5}}, \qquad C_K = \frac{64\,\pi^{4}\,m_e\,e^{10}} {3\sqrt{3}\,c\,h^{6}\,(4\pi\varepsilon_0)^{5}},\]
\[n_\mathrm{eff} = Z\sqrt{\frac{R_y}{I - E_i}}, \qquad R_y = \frac{m_e\,e^{4}}{8\,\varepsilon_0^{2}\,h^{2}}, \qquad Z = \texttt{chargeNumber} + 1 ,\]

with \(I\) the (unlowered) ionisation energy of the species and \(G\) the bound-free Gaunt factor (entry gauntFactor, default 1: the Kramers value; the database holds no referenced Gaunt factors). \(C_K = 2.815\times10^{25}\) m2 Hz3; for a hydrogen ground state (\(Z = 1\), \(n_\mathrm{eff} = 1\)) it gives 7.9 Mb at threshold (1 Mb = \(10^{-22}\) m2).

tabulated. Per level, from a photoionisation file (database/atoms/N_I_photoionisation.dat, O_I_photoionisation.dat), generated by database/sources/build_photoionisation.py from Johnston (2006), Appendix B (TOPbase fits). Each level takes one of three forms:

  • steps: a step model, \(\sigma\) constant on \([h\nu_A, h\nu_B)\) (Tables B-1, B-3). Below the first step (between the lowered threshold and the first tabulated energy) the first step value is used; above the last step, \(\sigma = 0\) (the end of Johnston’s tables, 18.9-19.0 eV);

  • power: the fit (Johnston 2006, Eq. 3.36)

    \[\sigma_i = \sigma_\mathrm{th} \left(\frac{h\nu_\mathrm{th}}{h\nu}\right)^{\theta}\]

    (Tables B-2, B-4), with no upper cut-off;

  • hydrogenic: the Kramers form above, for the levels outside Johnston’s level model.

Each level of the database takes the cross section of the Johnston level group it belongs to (the assignment is listed in the header of the data file).

Emission from absorption: the Milne relation

The code does not use a separate recombination cross section. It obtains the recombination emission from the photoionisation cross section by detailed balance level by level (the Milne relation, e.g. Rybicki and Lightman 1979). In equilibrium at \(T_e\), the population of level \(i\) in balance with the ion ground level and the free electrons is the Saha-Boltzmann value

\[S_i = n_e\,\frac{n_{+0}}{g_{+0}}\,\frac{g_i}{2}\,\Lambda^{3} \exp\!\left(\frac{E_b - E_i}{k T_e}\right), \qquad \Lambda^{3} = \left(\frac{h^{2}}{2\pi m_e k T_e}\right)^{3/2},\]

where \(n_{+0}\), \(g_{+0}\) are the population and degeneracy of the ion ground level (level 0 of the ionSpecies, from the population model), \(g_i\) the degeneracy of level \(i\), and the factor 2 the spin degeneracy of the free electron. The spectral coefficients per unit frequency are then (Johnston 2006, Eqs. 3.24-3.30, pp. 68-69)

\[j_\nu = \frac{2 h \nu^{3}}{c^{2}} \sum_i \sigma_i(\nu)\,S_i\,e^{-h\nu/kT_e},\]
\[\kappa_\nu = \sum_i \sigma_i(\nu) \left(n_i - S_i\,e^{-h\nu/kT_e}\right),\]

where the stimulated recombination is written as a negative absorption. Two checks follow directly:

  • if the populations are the Saha-Boltzmann ones (\(n_i = S_i\)), \(\kappa_\nu = \sum_i \sigma_i S_i (1 - e^{-h\nu/kT_e})\) and

    \[\frac{j_\nu}{\kappa_\nu} = \frac{2h\nu^{3}}{c^{2}} \frac{1}{e^{h\nu/kT_e} - 1} = B_\nu(T_e),\]

    so an optically thick, equilibrium layer radiates as a black body;

  • if the populations come from a nonequilibrium model (e.g. a DSMC cell distribution), the absorption uses the actual \(n_i\) while the emission depends only on the ion and the electrons, as it should for recombination. \(\kappa_\nu\) may then be negative (gain); it is kept with its sign.

The emission per unit wavelength is \(j_\lambda = j_\nu\,c/\lambda^2\); the absorption coefficient \(\kappa\) [m-1] is the same in both representations.

Free-free continuum

For an ion of charge \(Z\) (entry chargeNumber \(\ge 1\)) and number density \(n_+\) (the density of that species in the cell state), Kramers’ emission coefficient of Maxwellian electrons is (Johnston 2006, Eq. 3.32, p. 69; Zel’dovich and Raizer 1966; Kramers 1923), in SI:

\[j_\nu = C_{ff}\,Z^{2}\,G_{ff}\,n_e\,n_+\,T_e^{-1/2}\, e^{-h\nu/kT_e},\]
\[C_{ff} = \frac{8}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\, \frac{e^{6}}{(4\pi\varepsilon_0)^{3}\,m_e^{3/2}\,c^{3}\,k^{1/2}} = 5.444\times10^{-52}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{3}\,sr^{-1}\,Hz^{-1}\,K^{1/2}},\]

with \(G_{ff}\) the free-free Gaunt factor (entry gauntFreeFree, default 1). The absorption follows from Kirchhoff’s law. This is exact for Maxwellian electrons and needs no assumption on the bound levels, because both directions involve only free electrons:

\[\kappa_\nu = \frac{j_\nu}{B_\nu(T_e)} = C_{ff}\,Z^{2}\,G_{ff}\,n_e\,n_+\,T_e^{-1/2}\, \frac{c^{2}}{2h\nu^{3}}\left(1 - e^{-h\nu/kT_e}\right),\]

stimulated emission included. The code evaluates \(1 - e^{-x}\) as -expm1(-x), accurate for small \(x\). Electron-neutral bremsstrahlung is not included.

Constants

Both classes use the exact SI 2019 values of \(h\), \(k\), \(c\), \(e\) and the CODATA 2018 values of \(m_e\) and \(\varepsilon_0\), written in the class headers.

Outputs

In singleCell mode, an active continuum writes postProcessing/radiationFoam/continuum.csv with the emission and absorption of every process (named <model>_<species>, e.g. boundFreePhysical_N) and prints their integrals in the log. In shockTube1D mode the continuum is part of the total coefficients (Transport classes).
